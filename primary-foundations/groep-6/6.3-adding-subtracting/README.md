# 6.3 Adding and subtracting

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

In groep 6 adding and subtracting move onto paper with bigger numbers. Children first estimate (*schatten*), then calculate column by column (*kolomsgewijs*), and later in the year learn the standard written method (*cijferend*) with exchanging (*inwisselen*). Getting the order right matters: the column-by-column method builds understanding, and the written method is the fast routine that comes after.

## Dutch terms

> | Dutch | English |
> |---|---|
> | *optellen* / *erbij* / *plus* | add / plus |
> | *aftrekken* / *eraf* / *min* | subtract / minus |
> | *schatten* | estimate |
> | *kolomsgewijs* | column by column (left to right, writing part-answers) |
> | *cijferend* | standard written method (right to left, digit by digit) |
> | *inwisselen* | exchange (carrying and borrowing) |
> | *onthouden* | carry (literally "remember") |
> | *tekort* | shortfall (a negative part-answer in column subtraction) |
> | *verschil* | difference |
> | *controleren* | check |

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Please use the school's method, not the one you learned.** Ask the teacher which method the class is on now. In most groep 6 classes the order is: estimate → *kolomsgewijs* → *cijferend*.

**1. Estimate first (*schatten*).** Round to hundreds and calculate: 487 + 235 ≈ 500 + 200 = 700. After calculating, compare: "Is 722 close to 700? Good."

**2. Column by column (*kolomsgewijs*): adding.** Work from **left to right**, biggest place first, and write each part-answer on its own line:

```
    487
    235 +
   ----
    600     (400 + 200)
    110     ( 80 +  30)
     12     (  7 +   5)
   ---- +
    722
```

**3. Column by column: subtracting.** Again left to right. Many Dutch methods let a column "go below zero" and write the shortfall (*tekort*) with a minus sign:

```
    432
    263 −
   ----
    200     (400 − 200)
    −30     ( 30 −  60: 30 short)
     −1     (  2 −   3: 1 short)
   ----
    169     (200 − 30 − 1)
```

Other schools exchange (*inwisselen*) inside the column method instead. Ask which the class uses and do it that way.

**4. Standard written method (*cijferend*).** Work from **right to left**, one digit at a time. When adding gives 10 or more, write the ones and carry the ten as a small 1 above the next column (*onthouden*). When subtracting and the top digit is too small, exchange one from the next column (*inwisselen*): cross it out, write one less, and add 10 to the column you're working in.

```
  Adding 487 + 235               Subtracting 432 − 263

      1  1     ← carried            3  12  12   ← after exchanging
      4  8  7                       4   3   2
      2  3  5 +                     2   6   3 −
     --------                      ------------
      7  2  2                       1   6   9
```

In the subtraction: 2 − 3 can't be done, so exchange one ten (3 tens become 2, and 2 ones become 12): 12 − 3 = 9. Then 2 tens − 6 tens can't be done, so exchange one hundred (4 hundreds become 3, and 2 tens become 12): 12 − 6 = 6. Then 3 − 2 = 1.

Written exchanges look slightly different in each method; copy what's in his schoolbook.

**5. Check.** Compare with the estimate. For subtraction, add back: 169 + 263 = 432.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Subtracts the smaller digit from the bigger one regardless of position (432 − 263 → 231) | "Which number is on top? In the ones, we have 2 and we need to take away 3. Can we?" Use money: build €432 from €100, €10 and €1 slips and try to take away €263. |
| Forgets the carried 1 | "Where's the ten you swapped? Write it small above the next column straight away." |
| Exchanges across a zero (5 002 − 1 368) and gets stuck | This is the hardest case. Use play money: "There are no tens and no hundreds. Swap a thousand for 10 hundreds first." Go slowly. |
| Lines numbers up on the left instead of the right | Squared paper, one digit per square. Write H T E above the columns. |
| Wants to skip the estimate | "The estimate is your safety net. It catches big mistakes." Keep it quick, not a chore. |
| Panics when switching from *kolomsgewijs* to *cijferend* | Let him keep using *kolomsgewijs* at home until school has fully switched. Both are correct. |

## I can…

- estimate an answer before I work it out
- add big numbers column by column (*kolomsgewijs*)
- subtract big numbers column by column
- add the written way and exchange (*cijferend*, *inwisselen*)
- subtract the written way and exchange
- check my answer

## Suggested plan (4–5 weeks)

| Week | Sessions |
|---|---|
| 1 | Practice set 1 · Activity 1 (Estimate the shopping) · Practice set 2 · Game: Close to 1000 · Activity 2 (Money columns) |
| 2 | Practice set 3 · Game: Target 100 · Practice set 3 again with new numbers (you make them) · Activity 3 (Bike-trip planner) |
| 3 | Practice set 4 · Activity 4 (Spot the mistake) · Practice set 4, second half · Game: Close to 1000 |
| 4 | **Only when school has started *cijferend*:** Activity 2 again with exchanging · Practice set 5 (items 1–5) · Activity 5 (Change at the till) |
| 5 | Practice set 5 (items 6–10) · Puzzle corner · Update [PROGRESS.md](../../PROGRESS.md) |
