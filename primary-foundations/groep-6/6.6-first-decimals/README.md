# 6.6 First decimals

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

Decimals (*kommagetallen*) start in groep 6 with tenths and hundredths, closely linked to money (€2,45) and measuring (1,45 m). Children learn to place them on the number line and to compare them, and some methods go as far as comparing thousandths. He already uses decimals every time he reads a price, so this unit builds on something familiar.

## Dutch terms

> | Dutch | English |
> |---|---|
> | *kommagetal* | decimal number |
> | *komma* | decimal comma (in English: decimal point) |
> | *tiende(n)* | tenth(s) |
> | *honderdste(n)* | hundredth(s) |
> | *duizendste(n)* | thousandth(s) |
> | *heel getal* / *helen* | whole number / wholes |
> | *vergelijken* | compare |
> | *groter dan* / *kleiner dan* | greater than / less than |

## Parent in 2 minutes

- **The comma.** Dutch writes 0,5 and €2,45 with a decimal **comma**; English uses a point (0.5, €2.45). The practice sheets use the Dutch comma, because that's what he sees at school. Say this to him once, clearly: "In English it's a dot. In Dutch it's a comma. Same number."
- **Reading decimals.** School reads 2,45 as *twee komma vijfenveertig* (two comma forty-five). You can also say "2 wholes, 4 tenths and 5 hundredths", which is how school explains what it *means*.
- **Models school uses:** money (1 tenth of a euro = 10 cents, 1 hundredth = 1 cent), a metre stick (1 tenth of a metre = 10 cm, 1 hundredth = 1 cm), a 10 × 10 square grid, and the number line zooming in from 0–1 to 0–0,1.
- **The classic misconception:** "0,45 is bigger than 0,5 because 45 is bigger than 5". Money fixes it instantly: is €0,45 more than €0,50? Writing 0,5 as 0,50 (adding a zero at the end) is a good strategy.
- **Fraction link.** 0,5 = 1/2, 0,25 = 1/4, 0,75 = 3/4, 0,1 = 1/10. Connect to unit 6.5 using money: a quarter of a euro is 25 cents.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Thinks a longer decimal is bigger (0,125 > 0,5) | "Would you rather have €0,50 or €0,12 and a half cent?" Line up the commas and fill the gaps with zeros: 0,500 vs 0,125. |
| Thinks a shorter decimal is bigger (overcorrecting: 0,3 > 0,35) | Use the number line: 0,35 is halfway between 0,3 and 0,4. |
| Reads €12,05 as "twelve euros fifty" | "How many cents after the comma? The 0 is in the tenths place, so no tens of cents." Count coins. |
| Writes 2 m 8 cm as 2,8 m | "8 cm is 8 hundredths of a metre, so it goes in the hundredths place: 2,08." A metre stick helps. |
| Gets confused switching between comma and point | Stick to the comma for maths work. Mention the English point only when reading English sources. |

## I can…

- read and write tenths and hundredths
- link decimals to money and centimetres
- put decimals on a number line
- say which of two decimals is bigger
