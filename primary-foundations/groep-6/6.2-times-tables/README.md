# 6.2 Times tables

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

By groep 6, schools expect children to know the times tables (*tafels*) 1 to 10 quickly, because multiplying and dividing big numbers, fractions and ratio tables all build on them. This unit doesn't drill. It teaches a handful of **strategies** so that every fact can be *worked out* from a fact he already knows, and then uses games to make them quick. Speed comes last, and it comes from familiarity, not from a stopwatch.

## Parent in 2 minutes

- **School's approach.** Dutch methods teach tables through strategies first (doubling, using ×10 and ×5, "one more group" / "one fewer group") and only then practise for speed (*automatiseren*). The groep 6 learning line for *De wereld in getallen* states that speed practice should come only after the strategy is understood.
- **Notation.** Dutch schools write division with a colon: 24 : 6 = 4. Use the colon in practice with him.
- **There are fewer facts than it looks.** Once he knows ×1, ×2, ×5 and ×10, and that 6 × 7 is the same as 7 × 6 (*omdraaien*), only 21 facts are left in the 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 tables: 6 square facts (such as 7 × 7) and 15 others. Show him this: it's very reassuring.
- **The key strategies:**

| Table | Strategy |
|---|---|
| ×2 | Double. |
| ×4 | Double, then double again. |
| ×8 | Double, double, double. |
| ×10 | Put a 0 on the end (really: every digit moves one column left). |
| ×5 | Half of ×10. 5 × 8 = half of 80 = 40. |
| ×9 | ×10 then take one group away. 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63. |
| ×3 | ×2 plus one more group. |
| ×6 | ×5 plus one more group, or double ×3. |
| ×7 | ×5 plus ×2. 7 × 8 = 40 + 16 = 56. |
| any | A neighbour fact: 7 × 7 = 49, so 7 × 8 = 49 + 7 = 56. |
| ÷ | Think of the times table backwards: 56 : 7 = ? "7 times what is 56?" |

- **If the school does timed table tests** (many do, often online), ask the teacher whether he can do them untimed or with a calm "practice round" first. See the [parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md#7-working-with-the-teacher).

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Counts up from the start every time (7, 14, 21, 28…) | That's a real strategy, so don't ban it. Then offer a shortcut: "Do you know 5 × 7? Start from there." |
| Freezes on 7 × 8, 6 × 7, 6 × 8 | "What do you know that's close?" Let him choose the anchor fact. Write the anchor facts on a card he can keep. |
| Mixes up ×9 answers (9 × 7 = 64) | Check with the pattern: in the 9 table, the digits of the answer add up to 9 (6 + 3 = 9). |
| "I'm too slow" | "Fast isn't the goal. Knowing *how* to get there is. Speed comes on its own when you play the games." |
| Gets anxious when asked a fact out of the blue | Don't quiz at random moments (in the car, at dinner). Keep table practice inside games he has chosen. |

## I can…

- use doubling for the 2, 4 and 8 times tables
- use ×10 to work out ×5 and ×9
- use a fact I know to work out one I don't
- explain how I worked out a tricky one, like 7 × 8
- use a times table to work out a division

## Suggested plan (4 weeks, then games all year)

| Week | Sessions |
|---|---|
| 1 | Activity 1 (What I already know) · Practice set 1 · Activity 2 (Arrays) · Game: Multiplication bingo (×2, ×5, ×10) · Practice set 2 |
| 2 | Activity 3 (Doubling chains) · Game: Times-table snap (slow) with cards 1–5 and 10 · Practice set 3 · Activity 4 (Patterns on a 100-grid) |
| 3 | Practice set 4 (first half) · Game: Array capture · Practice set 4 (second half) · Activity 5 (My tricky-facts box) |
| 4 | Practice set 5 · Game: Yahtzee · Activity 6 (Tables in the shop) · Puzzle corner · Update [PROGRESS.md](../../PROGRESS.md) |
| After | One table game a week alongside the other units. Revisit the tricky-facts box every couple of weeks. |
