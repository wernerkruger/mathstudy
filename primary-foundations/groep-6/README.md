# Groep 6 (2026–27)

[← Back to the track](../README.md)

Groep 6 is a big year in Dutch primary maths. Numbers grow to 100,000, written methods for adding and subtracting start, multiplication and division move beyond the times tables, and fractions (*breuken*) and decimals (*kommagetallen*) appear properly for the first time. It is also the year when many children first feel that maths is "getting hard", so it is a very good year to rebuild confidence.

Before you start, read the [parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md), especially the sections on session structure and bad days.

## The units

| Unit | Main ideas | Domain | Suggested timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| [6.1 Numbers to 100,000](6.1-numbers-to-100000/) | Place value, number line, rounding | Numbers (*Getallen*) | 3 weeks |
| [6.2 Times tables](6.2-times-tables/) | Strategies (doubling, ×10, ×5, near facts), games, no timing | Numbers | 4 weeks, then keep playing the games all year |
| [6.3 Adding and subtracting](6.3-adding-subtracting/) | Estimating, column-by-column (*kolomsgewijs*), then the written method (*cijferend*) with exchanging (*inwisselen*) | Numbers | 4–5 weeks |
| [6.4 Multiplying and dividing](6.4-multiplying-dividing/) | Splitting, ×tens, column-by-column, division with remainder, estimating | Numbers | 4–5 weeks |
| [6.5 First fractions](6.5-first-fractions/) | Part of a whole, completing a whole, comparing, number line, fraction of an amount | Ratios (*Verhoudingen*) | 4 weeks |
| [6.6 First decimals](6.6-first-decimals/) | Tenths and hundredths, money and measuring, comparing | Ratios / Numbers | 3 weeks |
| [6.7 Measuring, time and money](6.7-measuring-time-money/) | Length, weight, capacity, perimeter, area, clocks, durations, calendar, money | Measurement and Geometry (*Meten en Meetkunde*) | 4–5 weeks |
| [6.8 Shapes, maps and graphs](6.8-shapes-maps-graphs/) | Coordinates and scale, floor plans, views, nets, pictograms and line graphs, ratio table intro | Measurement and Geometry; Relationships (*Verbanden*); Ratios | 3–4 weeks |

That's about 30–34 weeks at 15–20 minutes a day, 4–5 days a week, which fits from October 2026 to the end of June 2027 with room for holidays and rest weeks.

**Order.** The order follows the usual school sequence. Schools spread topics through the year in blocks, so the class will often be doing measuring or time between number topics. **Ask the teacher what is coming next and move units around to match.** Doing a topic at home a week or two *before* school teaches it is one of the best confidence boosters there is. Unit 6.2 (times tables) works well alongside any other unit: one times-table game a week all year.

**Pace.** These timings are a guide. If a unit is going well, move on. If it isn't, slow down, go back to the Warm-up sets and activities, and don't worry about falling "behind" this plan. The plan serves him, not the other way round.

## How the units map to school

Dutch primary schools organise arithmetic in four domains, which are also the four parts of the Cito pupil-tracking (LVS) arithmetic report:

| Domain | What it covers in groep 6 | Units |
|---|---|---|
| **Numbers** (*Getallen*) | Numbers to 100,000, place value, rounding, mental and written adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing | 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4 |
| **Ratios** (*Verhoudingen*) | Fractions, decimals, first ratio tables | 6.5, 6.6, 6.8 |
| **Measurement and Geometry** (*Meten en Meetkunde*) | Length, weight, capacity, perimeter, area, time, money, maps, floor plans, views, nets | 6.7, 6.8 |
| **Relationships** (*Verbanden*) | Tables, pictograms, line graphs | 6.8 (and graphs in 6.7) |

The content follows a typical groep 6 learning line, based on the published overview for *De wereld in getallen 5* ([PDF](https://www.onderwijsvanmorgen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/De-wereld-in-getallen-5.1-Visuele-leerlijn_Groep-6.pdf)). That overview includes, for example: numbers to 100,000 and rounding; estimating, then column-by-column, then the written method for adding and subtracting (487 + 235; 432 − 263; 1705 − 346); multiplying by splitting (4 × 67), with round numbers (3 × 700, 30 × 40), column-by-column (6 × 284) and the written method (4 × 536); dividing by splitting (92 : 4), with round numbers (1500 : 30) and with a remainder (826 : 9); first fractions and decimals; the ratio table; measuring, time to the second, maps, views and nets; pictograms and line graphs. It also notes that speed practice for facts (*automatiseren*) only comes after the strategy is understood. Other methods cover similar content in a different order.

**New national goals.** New national goals (*kerndoelen*) for arithmetic and maths became law in August 2026, and schools have until August 2031 to switch. They put more weight on reasoning, estimating and checking, choosing how to calculate, and real-life maths such as pocket money and discounts ([what changes](https://www.actualisatiekerndoelen.nl/rekenenwiskunde/updates/nieuwe-kerndoelen-rekenen-en-wiskunde-wat-verandert-er)). The units here already include a lot of estimating, checking and "talk about it", so they fit both the old and the new goals.

## Pupil-tracking tests this year

Most schools test arithmetic around January–February and again around June. Please don't prepare specially. See [section 8 of the parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md#8-pupil-tracking-tests-lvs--cito-and-the-doorstroomtoets) for how to talk about tests and results calmly.

## A typical week

| Day | Session (15–20 min) |
|---|---|
| Monday | Activity from the current unit |
| Tuesday | One practice set (start with Warm-up) |
| Wednesday | A game from [GAMES.md](../GAMES.md), often a times-table game |
| Thursday | Activity or practice set |
| Friday | Free choice: game, puzzle corner or real-life maths. Update [PROGRESS.md](../PROGRESS.md) if he wants to. |
| Weekend | Nothing formal. Maths in daily life if it comes up naturally: cooking, shopping, train times. |

## Before you start

Ask the teacher (see [section 7 of the parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md#7-working-with-the-teacher)):

1. Which maths method and which block are they on?
2. Do they use column-by-column (*kolomsgewijs*) or the written method (*cijferend*) for adding and subtracting right now?
3. Are times-table tests timed?
4. Could he have a *Rekengesprek*?
