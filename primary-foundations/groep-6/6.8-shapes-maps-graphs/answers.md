## Puzzle corner

There are **11** different cube nets. Six of them are "a row of 4 with one square above and one below" in different positions; the other five are different shapes, including the "staircase":

```
+--+--+
|  |  |
+--+--+--+
   |  |  |
   +--+--+--+
      |  |  |
      +--+--+
```

Finding one new net is a real success; three is excellent. Proving there are exactly 11 is genuinely hard (adults find it hard too). The key ideas are: never four squares in a 2 × 2 block, and never five in a row. If he gets organised (for example "nets with a row of 4", then "nets with a longest row of 3", then "longest row of 2"), that's mathematical thinking at its best.
