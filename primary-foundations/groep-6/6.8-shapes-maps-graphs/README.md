# 6.8 Shapes, maps and graphs

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

This unit brings together the "picture" side of maths: finding places on a map with coordinates (*coördinaten*) and using a scale (*schaal*), reading a floor plan (*plattegrond*), matching a building to its front, side and top views (*aanzichten*), recognising the net of a cube (*bouwplaat*), reading pictograms (*beelddiagram*) and line graphs (*lijndiagram*), and a first look at the ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*). Many children who feel anxious about sums enjoy this kind of maths, so it can be a real confidence boost.

## Parent in 2 minutes

- **Coordinates in groep 6** are grid squares on a map: a letter for the column and a number for the row (B2), like on a town map or in an atlas. Point coordinates (*assenstelsel*) come later.
- **Scale.** In groep 6 the scale is usually given as "1 cm on the map = 1 km in real life" or as a scale bar. The ratio notation (1 : 100 000) comes in groep 7–8. A ratio table is the school's tool for working with scale.
- **Views.** School uses small block buildings and asks: what do you see from the front, side, and top? Often the top view is drawn with numbers showing how many blocks are stacked on each square. Real blocks or Lego are much better than drawings here.
- **Nets.** There are exactly 11 different nets of a cube. Children learn to spot which shapes fold into a cube. Cutting and folding paper is the best way to check.
- **Graphs.** Pictograms use a symbol for a number of things (one bike symbol = 10 bikes). Line graphs show something changing over time (temperature during the day). The new national goals ask children to read graphs **critically**: "Does this graph make the difference look bigger than it is?"
- **Ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*).** A two-row table where both rows change in the same way: double the top, double the bottom. It's the school's main tool for recipes, prices, scale and later percentages. Introduce it as "a smart way to keep track", not as a new rule.

```
| packs  | 1 | 2  | 4  | 8  |
| drinks | 6 | 12 | 24 | 48 |
           ×2   ×2   ×2
```

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Reads coordinates the wrong way round (2B) or finds the row instead of the column | "Letter first, then number. Go along the corridor, then up (or down) the stairs." Run a finger along. |
| Measures a map distance but forgets to use the scale | "That's how long it is on the paper. How far is it really?" Make a mini ratio table. |
| Can't imagine the side view | Build it with blocks and literally crouch down to look from the side. Then draw. |
| Thinks any 6 joined squares make a cube | "Let's cut it out and try." Folding settles it without argument. |
| Reads each picture in a pictogram as 1 | "Look at the key: what is one picture worth?" |
| Fills a ratio table by adding the same number on both rows (1→2 on top, 6→7 below) | "If you buy twice as many packs, how many drinks do you get?" Use real packs. |

## I can…

- find a place on a map using coordinates
- use a map scale to work out a real distance
- read a floor plan (*plattegrond*)
- match front, side and top views to a building
- choose the net (*bouwplaat*) of a cube
- read a pictogram and a line graph
- fill in a ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*)
