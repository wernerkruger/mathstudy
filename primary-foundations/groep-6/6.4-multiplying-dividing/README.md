# 6.4 Multiplying and dividing

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

This unit takes the times tables (unit 6.2) into bigger numbers: 4 × 67, 30 × 40, 6 × 284, 92 : 4 and divisions with a remainder (*rest*). The main tool is **splitting** (*splitsen*) a number into easy parts, which is how school teaches it and how most adults calculate in their heads. Estimating keeps answers sensible.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Multiplying by splitting (*splitsen*).** Split the bigger number into tens and ones:

```
4 × 67  =  4 × 60  +  4 × 7  =  240 + 28  =  268
```

**Multiplying with round numbers.** Use the table fact and think about place value: 3 × 700 is "3 × 7 hundreds = 21 hundreds = 2 100". 30 × 40 is "3 × 4 = 12, and tens times tens gives hundreds: 1 200". Please explain it as hundreds and tens rather than "just add the zeros", because the zero rule breaks later with decimals.

**Column by column (*kolomsgewijs*).** The same as splitting, written vertically, biggest part first:

```
     284
       6 ×
    ----
    1200     (6 × 200)
     480     (6 ×  80)
      24     (6 ×   4)
    ---- +
    1704
```

The standard written method (*cijferend*, right to left with carrying, e.g. 4 × 536) usually comes at the end of groep 6. Only practise it at home once school has started it.

**Dividing by splitting.** Split the number into parts that are easy to divide:

```
92 : 4  →  80 : 4 = 20   and   12 : 4 = 3   →   20 + 3 = 23
```

**Dividing with round numbers.** 1 500 : 30 → "how many 30s in 1 500?" → 150 : 3 = 50.

**Dividing with a remainder, "taking bites" (*happen*).** Take big, easy chunks:

```
826 : 9
  9 × 90 = 810     826 − 810 = 16 left
  9 ×  1 =   9      16 −   9 =  7 left (too small for another 9)
  90 + 1 = 91       answer: 91 rest 7
```

**The remainder in real life.** In word problems the answer depends on the question: 58 children, 4 per car: 14 rest 2, so you need **15** cars (round up). 75 eggs in boxes of 6: 12 rest 3, so **12** full boxes (round down). This is a reasoning skill the new national goals stress.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| 4 × 67 = 4 × 60 + 7 = 247 (forgets to multiply the ones) | "Four groups of 67. Each group has 60 *and* 7. How many 7s do we have?" Draw 4 boxes, each with "60 + 7". |
| 30 × 40 = 120 | "3 tens times 4 tens. Is 30 × 40 more or less than 30 × 10?" (30 × 10 = 300, so 120 can't be right.) |
| Loses track in *kolomsgewijs* | Write the small calculation next to each line, in brackets, like in the example. |
| Doesn't know where to start dividing | "What's an easy chunk? Can you do 10 times? 20 times?" |
| The remainder is bigger than the divisor (826 : 9 = 90 rest 16) | "Can another 9 fit into 16?" A remainder must be smaller than the number you divide by. |
| Writes "23 rest 0" or gets upset by remainders | Remainders are normal: real life rarely shares out exactly. |

## I can…

- multiply by splitting a number, like 4 × 67
- multiply with tens and hundreds, like 30 × 40
- multiply a 3-digit number on paper
- divide by splitting, like 92 : 4
- divide and say what to do with the remainder
- estimate an answer to check it makes sense
