## Set 3 · Which is bigger?

| # | Answer | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1/2 | Shared by 2 gives bigger pieces than shared by 4. |
| 2 | 1/3 | Thirds are bigger than fifths. |
| 3 | 3/4 | Same size pieces (quarters); 3 is more than 1. |
| 4 | 4/5 | Same size pieces (fifths); 4 is more than 2. |
| 5 | 1/6 | Sixths are bigger than eighths. |
| 6 | = (the same) | Two quarters fill exactly the same as one half. |
| 7 | 5/6 | 5/6 is more than half (half would be 3/6). |
| 8 | Lina | 1/3 is bigger than 1/4. |
| 9 | 1 | 9/10 is one tenth short of a whole. |
| 10 | = (the same) | Two halves make one whole. |

**Talk about it:** when the top number is 1, the **bigger** the bottom number, the **smaller** the piece, because the whole is shared between more.

## Puzzle corner

- **Three unit fractions that make 1:** 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3; 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4; 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6. These are the only three ways with three unit fractions.
- **Different unit fractions:** 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1. With four: 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 1, or 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/5 + 1/20 = 1.
- **Big challenge:** there are infinitely many ways once you allow more fractions. (Any 1/n can be split into 1/(n+1) + 1/(n × (n+1)), for example 1/6 = 1/7 + 1/42.) That's far beyond groep 6, but if he notices "I can keep going", that's real mathematical thinking.

The strips can check 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 directly: lay a half, a third and a sixth end to end under the whole strip.
