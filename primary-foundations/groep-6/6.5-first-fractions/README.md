# 6.5 First fractions

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

Fractions (*breuken*) are introduced properly in groep 6: a fraction as part of a whole, what's missing to make a whole (*aanvullen tot een geheel*), comparing fractions, fractions on the number line, and a fraction of an amount (¼ of 20). Fractions are one of the topics children most often say "I don't get", so a calm, hands-on start with strips, pizzas and money pays off for years. Calculating with fractions comes in groep 7.

## Parent in 2 minutes

- **Models school uses:** fraction strips (*breukenstroken*), pizzas or circles, chocolate bars, the number line, and money (¼ of €1 = 25 cents). Use the same ones.
- **Equal parts matter.** A shape cut into 4 *unequal* pieces is not in quarters. Ask about this often.
- **Saying fractions in Dutch.** ¾ is *drie vierde* or *driekwart*, ⅖ is *twee vijfde*. Note: Dutch uses *vierde*, *vijfde* (like "fourth", "fifth") for the bottom number, and a half is *een half* / *de helft*.
- **The big surprise:** a bigger bottom number means a *smaller* piece. ⅙ is less than ⅓. Children often expect the opposite because 6 > 3. Strips and pizzas make this obvious.
- **Fraction of an amount:** ¼ of 20 = 20 divided into 4 equal groups = 5. ¾ of 20 = three of those groups = 15. Schools often draw a strip or bar split into parts above the amount.
- **The whole from a part (*geheel uit deel*):** "¼ of the bag is 6 sweets. How many in the whole bag?" 4 × 6 = 24. Draw the strip.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Thinks ⅛ is bigger than ¼ "because 8 is bigger" | "Would you rather have a pizza shared by 8 people or by 4 people?" Fold two strips. |
| Counts parts but ignores whether they're equal | "Are all the pieces the same size? Would you be happy to get that piece?" |
| Writes the shaded parts over the unshaded parts (3 shaded, 1 not → ³⁄₁) | "The bottom number tells how many pieces the *whole* is cut into. How many altogether?" |
| Gets ¾ of 20 wrong by doing 20 : 3 or 20 : 4 only | "First find one quarter. Then how many quarters do you need?" |
| Gets anxious when he sees fraction symbols | Go back to words and objects for a few sessions. "A quarter of the pizza" before ¼. |

## I can…

- show a fraction as part of a shape or strip
- say how much is missing to make a whole
- say which of two fractions is bigger
- put fractions on a number line
- find a fraction of an amount, like ¼ of 20
