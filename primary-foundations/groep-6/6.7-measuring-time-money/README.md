# 6.7 Measuring, time and money

[← Groep 6](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

## What and why

This unit covers the everyday maths of measuring: length (*lengte*), weight (*gewicht*) and capacity (*inhoud*) and converting between units; perimeter (*omtrek*) and area (*oppervlakte*); telling the time on analogue and digital clocks, working out durations and using a calendar; and money. It's the most practical unit of the year, and the one where home can help most, because the kitchen, the bike and the train timetable are full of it.

> | Dutch | English |

> | *lengte*, *gewicht*, *inhoud* | length, weight, capacity |
> | *omrekenen* | convert |

> | *oppervlakte* | area (how much surface inside) |
> | *vierkante centimeter (cm²)*, *vierkante meter (m²)* | square centimetre, square metre |
> | *klokkijken*, *analoge klok*, *digitale klok* | telling the time, analogue clock, digital clock |

> | *tijdsduur* | duration |
> | *kalender*, *schrikkeljaar* | calendar, leap year |
> | *wisselgeld* | change (money) |

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Units groep 6 uses:**

| Length | Weight | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 km = 1 000 m | 1 kg = 1 000 g | 1 l = 10 dl = 100 cl = 1 000 ml |
| 1 m = 10 dm = 100 cm | | |
| 1 cm = 10 mm | | |

Many schools use a "metric staircase" (*metriek trapje*) for converting. In groep 6 it's mostly used for length; the full system comes in groep 7. Use benchmarks: a door is about 2 m tall, a pack of butter is 250 g, a milk carton is 1 l, a can of drink is 330 ml.

**Telling the time in Dutch is different from English.** This matters if you're an English-speaking parent:

| Time | Dutch | English |
|---|---|---|
| 3:30 | *half vier* ("half four") | half past three |
| 4:20 | *tien voor half vijf* ("ten before half five") | twenty past four |
| 4:40 | *tien over half vijf* ("ten after half five") | twenty to five |
| 7:15 | *kwart over zeven* | quarter past seven |
| 7:45 | *kwart voor acht* | quarter to eight |

In Dutch, "half" points **forward** to the next hour. So *half vier* is 3:30, not 4:30. Use the Dutch phrases with him for clock work, because that's what school tests. Digital times use the 24-hour clock (19:50 = *tien voor acht 's avonds*).

**Durations.** School uses a time line (like a number line): from 18:45 jump to 19:00 (15 min), then to 20:00 (1 hour), then to 20:10 (10 min). Total 1 hour 25 min. Avoid column subtraction for times: minutes don't work in base 10.

**Perimeter and area.** Perimeter = walk around the edge (a fence). Area = cover the inside (a carpet, tiles). On squared paper, count the squares for area and the edges for perimeter before any formula.

**Money.** In Dutch shops, cash totals are usually rounded to the nearest 5 cents. Card payments are exact. Change is usually worked out by counting up.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| Stumbling block | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Says *half vier* is 4:30 | "In Dutch, 'half' means halfway *to* four." Show it on a real clock. |
| Treats time like ordinary numbers: 10:47 to 11:32 = 85 minutes (1132 − 1047) | "An hour has 60 minutes, not 100." Use the time line: 10:47 → 11:00 is 13 min, → 11:32 is 32 min more. |
| Mixes up perimeter and area | "Perimeter: an ant walking around the edge. Area: paint covering the inside." Act it out with a finger. |
| Converts the wrong way (3 m = 0,03 cm) | "Is a centimetre bigger or smaller than a metre? So do you need more or fewer of them?" |
| Can't estimate weights or lengths at all | Kitchen-scale and tape-measure games. Benchmarks first, calculations second. |

## I can…

- change between km, m, cm and mm
- change between kg and g, and between l, dl and ml
- work out the perimeter and area of a rectangle
- read analogue and digital clocks
- work out how long something takes
- use a calendar
- pay and work out change
