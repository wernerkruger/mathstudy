# 6.7 Measuring, time and money: activities

[← Unit overview](README.md)

## 1. Kitchen measuring

**You need:** kitchen scales, a measuring jug, a simple recipe (pancakes work well).

1. Read the recipe together. Which units are used? (g, ml, dl, a spoon.)
2. He weighs and measures the ingredients. Ask before each one: "Will 250 g of flour look like a lot or a little?"
3. Convert as you go: "The recipe says 0,5 l of milk. How many ml is that? Where's that on the jug?"
4. Eat the pancakes. (This is essential.)

**Talk about it:** "Which was heavier, the flour or the milk? Did you guess right?"

## 2. Measure the house

**You need:** a tape measure, squared paper, a pencil.

1. Measure his bedroom: length and width in metres (round to whole or half metres).
2. Draw it on squared paper: 1 square = 1 m (or ½ m).
3. **Perimeter:** "If we put a ribbon all the way round the edge of the floor, how long would it be?"
4. **Area:** "How many 1 m × 1 m squares fit on the floor?" Count them on the drawing.
5. Compare with another room. Which room has the bigger area? The bigger perimeter? Are they the same room?

**Talk about it:** "Can a room have a bigger perimeter but a smaller area than another room?"

## 3. Dutch clock talk

**You need:** an analogue clock (or a paper plate clock with a split-pin), a phone showing a digital clock.

1. Set times on the analogue clock. He says them in Dutch (*kwart over drie*, *tien voor half vijf*) and writes the digital time.
2. Swap: you say a digital time in the 24-hour clock (16:20); he sets the analogue clock and says it in Dutch.
3. Include morning and evening: "Is 19:50 in the morning or the evening? What would you say in Dutch?"

**Talk about it:** "Why do you think Dutch says *half vier* for 3:30?"

## 4. Train trip planner

**You need:** the NS app or a printed train timetable.

Plan a real or pretend day trip. When does the train leave? When does it arrive? How long is the journey? If there's a change of train, how long is the wait? What time do you need to leave home if cycling to the station takes 12 minutes?

**Talk about it:** "Which is quicker, the direct train or the one with a change? How did you work it out?"

## 5. Shop with change

**You need:** coins and notes (real or play), price labels with cents.

He is the shopkeeper. You buy two or three items (€1,29, €2,15, €3,49), pay with a €10 note, and he gives change by counting up. Swap roles and make a deliberate mistake for him to catch.

Bonus: explain the Dutch 5-cent rounding when paying cash. "€6,93 is rounded to €6,95 if you pay with coins."

**Talk about it:** "Why do shops count up to give change instead of subtracting?"

## 6. Family calendar

**You need:** a wall calendar or a printed year calendar.

- How many days until his birthday? Until the next school holiday?
- Which months have 31 days? (The knuckle trick: make two fists; knuckles are 31-day months, the dips between them are shorter months.)
- "What day of the week will it be in 10 days? In 3 weeks?"
- "Is 2028 a leap year (*schrikkeljaar*)? How do you know?" (Yes, it can be divided by 4.)

**Talk about it:** "Why do you think February is shorter?"

## Conversation prompts (any time)

- "About how tall is that tree? How did you estimate?"
- "Which weighs more: a kilo of feathers or a kilo of bricks?"
- "How long do you think it takes to cycle to school? Let's check tomorrow."
- "What time will it be in 45 minutes? How did you work that out?"
