# 8.6 The doorstroomtoets, calmly

[← Groep 8 overview](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

The end-of-primary test (*doorstroomtoets*) is the one moment in primary school that can feel like a big exam. For an anxious child, the worry about the test can do more harm than the test itself: stress takes up working memory, and working memory is exactly what maths needs.

This unit has three aims:

1. Make sure he (and you) know what the test **is and isn't**, so it shrinks to its real size.
2. Make every question type familiar, practised first without any time pressure.
3. Give him a calm routine for the weeks before and for the test days themselves.

It does **not** try to drill for a higher score. The best preparation for the arithmetic part is the work in units 8.1–8.5, done calmly. This unit is about letting that work show.

## What the test is, and what it isn't

**What it is** ([Cito information for parents](https://cito.nl/onderwijs/primair-onderwijs/informatie-voor-ouders/over-de-doorstroomtoets/); [Cito factsheet](https://cito.nl/media/ewnpxwqo/cito-factsheet-doorstroomtoets.pdf)):

- A national test that every child in groep 8 takes, around **late January to mid-February 2029** (projected from the 2026–27 calendar: test window 25 January–12 February, with a catch-up period after).
- It covers **reading** (*lezen*), **spelling and grammar** (*taalverzorging*) and **arithmetic** (*rekenen*). Maths questions are a mix of open and multiple-choice.
- It measures against fixed levels: **1F** (the fundamental level) and **1S/2F** (the target level). See the [groep 8 overview](../README.md#the-four-domains).
- In 2026–27 schools could choose from five approved tests (Dia, IEP, Cito's Leerling in beeld, Route 8 and the government's DOE). A single national test may come around 2029–30. Ask the school in groep 7 or early groep 8 which one he will take, on paper or on screen.
- It gives a **test advice** (*toetsadvies*) for the type of secondary school.

**What it isn't:**

- **It is not the decision.** The school's advice (*schooladvies*) is based on years of work, not one test. The school gives a **provisional advice** (*voorlopig advies*) before the test, around January 2029.
- **It can only help, never hurt.** If the test advice comes out **higher** than the provisional advice, the school must reconsider and may raise it. If it comes out **lower**, the provisional advice stays. This is the most important fact to share with him.
- **It is not a pass/fail exam.** Nobody "fails" the doorstroomtoets.
- **It is not something to cram for.** Schools prepare children for the format. A few weeks of calm, familiar practice is plenty.

### How to say it to him

> "In January everyone in groep 8 does a test called the doorstroomtoets. Before the test, your teacher already gives you an advice about which secondary school suits you, based on everything she's seen these years. The test can only make that advice go up, never down. So it's a chance, not a danger. All you need to do is show what you can do, the same as on a normal day."

Say it once, calmly, and then don't keep bringing it up. Let him ask questions when he wants.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Your mood is the biggest factor you control.** Research on maths anxiety finds that when maths-anxious parents help a lot with homework, children learn less and become more anxious themselves ([Maloney et al., 2015](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/parents-math-anxiety-can-undermine-childrens-math-achievement.html)). In the test weeks, sound bored about the test and interested in him.

**Practice in "test style", but without timers first.** Familiar question formats lower stress. The practice sets here look like test questions (mixed topics, some multiple choice, a domain label), but there is **no clock** until the very last week, and even then only a relaxed "see how it feels" session if he wants one. Timed tests are a known trigger for maths anxiety ([Boaler, *Fluency Without Fear*](https://www.youcubed.org/evidence/fluency-without-fear/)).

**Use Cito's sample test once, together.** Cito publishes a sample test (*voorbeeldtoets*) for its own doorstroomtoets on paper and online ([voorbeeldmateriaal](https://cito.nl/onderwijs/primair-onderwijs/leerling-in-beeld-doorstroomtoets/ontdek-de-doorstroomtoets/voorbeeldmateriaal/)). Look through it together around four weeks before the test, mostly to see what it looks like. If his school uses a different test, ask the teacher whether there is sample material for that one.

**Skill beats pep talks.** Reviews of maths-anxiety interventions find that building real skill reduces anxiety **and** raises ability, while calming techniques alone lower anxiety but don't raise ability ([Balt et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.798516/full)). So do both: calm routines **and** the steady practice from earlier units. Praise specific actions ("You read that question twice before answering, good") rather than general "you're so clever" statements.

## The six-week plan

Projected for a test around late January 2029. Move it to fit the real date. Keep sessions to 15–20 minutes.

| Week | When (approx.) | Focus | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mid-December 2028 | **Talk and look.** Explain what the test is and isn't (above). Make the "I can" list (activity 1). | 2–3 short sessions: Practice Set 1, items 1–5 only. Games. |
| 2 | Christmas holiday | **Rest, mostly.** Games from [GAMES.md](../../GAMES.md) and real-life maths (cooking, shopping). | 1–2 relaxed sessions: rest of Set 1. |
| 3 | Early January 2029 | **Mixed practice, no clock.** | Set 2, half a set per session. Activity 3 (skip and come back). |
| 4 | Mid-January | **Meet the real format.** Look at the Cito sample test together (activity 2). | Set 3 over 2–3 sessions. The provisional advice may arrive around now: talk about it calmly. |
| 5 | About a week before | **Rehearsal.** One "test-style morning" with Set 4 in one sitting, still no timer (activity 4). | Then only light sessions. Try the calm kit (activity 5). |
| 6 | Test week | **Nothing new.** No worksheets. | Games, early nights, the test-day routine below. |

If he is worried during the plan, **do less, not more**. Missing a week of practice costs nothing; adding stress costs a lot.

## Test-day routine

**The evening before:** a normal evening, a normal bedtime. Pack a pencil case if the school asks (pencils, eraser, ruler). No practice.

**The morning:** a normal breakfast. Arrive on time, not early-and-waiting. A short, light goodbye: "Have a good morning. See you at lunch." Avoid "Good luck!" said with a worried face; a smile and something ordinary is better.

**During the test, his own toolkit** (practise these in weeks 3–5):

- **Breathe first.** Two slow breaths before the first question: in through the nose for 4, out through the mouth for 6.
- **Read the question twice.** Underline what is asked.
- **Skip and come back.** If a question is hard, mark it, move on, and return at the end. A fresh look often helps.
- **Draw it.** Strips, tables and number lines are allowed on scrap paper.
- **Estimate to check.** "Does my answer make sense?"
- **Never leave a multiple-choice question blank.** Cross out what can't be right, then choose.
- **If your mind goes blank:** pencil down, breathe, read the question again slowly, start with what you **do** know.

**After each test day:** no post-mortem. Don't ask "How did it go? What did you get for question 12?" Ask about lunch or friends. Do something nice together. If he wants to talk about it, listen.

## Managing nerves

Some nerves are normal and even useful. The aim is not "no nerves" but "nerves that don't take over".

- **Name it.** "Your tummy feels funny because your body is getting ready. It's not a sign that something is wrong."
- **Breathing.** Slow out-breaths calm the body. Practise at home when calm, so it's automatic when needed.
- **A short "I can" list.** Written by him in week 1 (activity 1), read on test morning.
- **Optional: write your worries down.** Some research found that students who spent about 10 minutes writing freely about their worries just before a test did better than those who didn't (Ramirez & Beilock, 2011, published in *Science*; see the [parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md)). **Important caveat:** this was tested with older students (secondary school and university), not with children his age. It may or may not help him. If you try it, try it at home before a normal school test first, keep it private (he can throw the paper away), and drop it if it makes him more worried. See activity 6.
- **Keep the rest of life normal.** Sport, play, friends and sleep matter more in these weeks than extra practice.

If the worry is strong (trouble sleeping, stomach aches, tears about school), talk to the teacher or the school's support coordinator (*intern begeleider*). Schools know this well and can help, for example with a quieter room.

## When the result comes

Results reach the school by about mid-March. Whatever the result:

- Thank him for doing it, not for the score.
- If the test advice is higher than the provisional one, the school will reconsider: that's good news to share.
- If it is the same or lower, nothing changes: the provisional advice stands. Say so plainly: "Your advice stays the same. It was already a good fit for you."
- Avoid comparing with classmates or siblings.

## "I can…" goals

- I can explain what the doorstroomtoets is, and that it can only help my school advice.
- I have seen every kind of maths question that can come up.
- I can use my test toolkit: breathe, read twice, skip and come back, draw it, estimate to check.
- I know what I'll do if my mind goes blank.
