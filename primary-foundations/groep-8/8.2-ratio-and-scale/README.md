# 8.2 Ratio and scale

[← Groep 8 overview](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

A ratio (*verhouding*) says how two amounts belong together: 1 part syrup to 4 parts water, 4 eggs for 8 pancakes, 1 cm on the map for 250 m in real life, 15 km in 1 hour. In groep 8 children use the same tool, the **ratio table** (*verhoudingstabel*), for all of these: recipes, maps and scale (*schaal*), speed (*snelheid*) in km per hour (*km/u*), and finding the best buy in the supermarket.

This is the domain *Verhoudingen* on the doorstroomtoets, and it is also the most "real-life" maths in groep 8. For an anxious child that is a gift: almost every problem can be acted out with a measuring jug, a map or a shopping list.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**How school teaches it.** Almost everything goes through a ratio table. You write the two things that belong together in two rows, then multiply or divide **both rows by the same number**. You can also add two columns together.

```
  pakken  |  1  |  3  |  6
  euro    | 2,50| 7,50| 15,00
```

**Scale (*schaal*).** "1 : 25 000" means 1 cm on the map is 25 000 cm in real life. School teaches children to turn that into metres or km straight away: 25 000 cm = 250 m. So 4 cm on the map is 1 km. Most Dutch walking and cycling maps are 1 : 25 000 or 1 : 50 000; a topographic map is often 1 : 25 000.

| Scale | 1 cm on the map is… |
|---|---|
| 1 : 100 | 1 m |
| 1 : 1 000 | 10 m |
| 1 : 25 000 | 250 m |
| 1 : 50 000 | 500 m |
| 1 : 100 000 | 1 km |

**Speed (*snelheid*).** "15 km/u" is a ratio: 15 km in 60 minutes. In a ratio table you halve both: 7,5 km in 30 minutes; divide by 4: 3,75 km in 15 minutes. Minutes and hours on the top row, km on the bottom.

**Best buy.** Bring both offers to the same amount (per kilo, per litre, per 100 g, or per item), then compare. School usually does this with a ratio table too.

**What to say when he's stuck:** "What belongs together? Put them in a table. What could you do to both rows?"

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What is probably going on | What to say or do |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplies only one row of the table | Hasn't yet felt that the pair must stay "fair" | "If 1 pack is €2,50, can 3 packs still be €2,50?" Act it out with real packs. |
| Adds instead of multiplying (recipe for 4 → 6 people: adds 2 g of everything) | Additive thinking | "Is 6 people 2 more, or one and a half times as many?" Use a halfway column: for 2, then for 6. |
| 1 : 25 000 → says "4 cm is 100 000 km" | Lost the units | Always change to metres first: 25 000 cm = 250 m. Write it at the top of the page. |
| Mixes up minutes as decimals (1,5 hours = 1 h 50 min) | Decimal hours aren't like clock time | "Half an hour is how many minutes?" Draw a clock face. 1,5 h = 1 h 30 min. |
| Picks the bigger pack automatically as "best buy" | Rule of thumb, not checked | "Let's check per kilo." Sometimes the small one wins. |

## "I can…" goals

- I can use a ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*) to scale a recipe up or down.
- I can say what 1 cm on a map means for scales such as 1 : 25 000 and 1 : 50 000.
- I can work out a real distance from a map, and a map distance from a real one.
- I can work out how far you go at a given speed in km/u, and how fast something went.
- I can compare two offers and say which is the best buy, and explain why.
