# 8.1 Fractions, decimals and percentages

[← Groep 8 overview](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

One number can wear three outfits: ½ is the same as 0,5 is the same as 50%. In groep 8 school puts fractions (*breuken*), decimals (*kommagetallen*) and percentages (*procenten*) side by side and expects children to switch between them, compare them, and use percentages with money: discounts (*korting*), VAT (*btw*) and price rises.

This matters for two reasons. It is one of the biggest topics on the doorstroomtoets (in the domains *Getallen* and *Verhoudingen*). And it is the maths of daily life: sales, pocket money (*zakgeld*), scores on a test. The new national goals (*kerndoelen*) put extra weight on exactly these everyday uses.

For an anxious child the good news is that a small set of "anchor" facts (½, ¼, ¾, 1/10, 1/5 and 1/100) does most of the work. If he knows those as friends, almost every question becomes "find 10% (or 50%, or 1%) first, then build".

## Parent in 2 minutes

**How school teaches it.** Most Dutch methods use two models. The first is the **percentage strip** (*procentenstrook*): a long bar with 0% at one end and 100% at the other, with the real amount written underneath. For "25% of €80" the child draws a bar, marks 100% = €80, halves to 50% = €40, halves again to 25% = €20. The second is the **ratio table** (*verhoudingstabel*): a two-row table with % on top and the amount below, where you divide or multiply both rows by the same number.

```
  %   | 100 | 10 |  5 | 15
  €   |  80 |  8 |  4 | 12
```

**The key moves:**

- **10% = divide by 10.** Then 5% is half of that, 20% is double, 15% is 10% + 5%.
- **1% = divide by 100.** Useful for awkward percentages such as 3% or 21%.
- **Fraction to percentage:** make the denominator 100 (3/5 = 60/100 = 60%), or use the anchors (1/5 = 20%, so 3/5 = 60%).
- **Discount (*korting*):** work out the discount, then subtract it. "30% korting on €70": 10% = €7, 30% = €21, new price €49. A shortcut for later: you pay 70%.
- **VAT (*btw*):** the standard rate in the Netherlands is 21%, with a low rate of 9% on things like food. "Price excl. btw €100" means you add 21%: €121.
- **Percentage increase:** compare the change with the **old** amount. From €40 to €50 is €10 more; €10 out of €40 is ¼ = 25%.

**What to say when he's stuck:** "What is 100% here? Can you find 10%?" That one question unlocks most problems.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What is probably going on | What to say or do |
|---|---|---|
| Thinks 0,25 is bigger than 0,5 "because 25 is bigger than 5" | Treating decimals like whole numbers | "Put them in money: €0,25 or €0,50?" Fill in a zero: 0,25 vs 0,50. |
| Says 1/8 is bigger than 1/4 "because 8 is bigger" | Bigger denominator feels like more | "Would you rather have a pizza cut into 4 or 8 and get one slice?" Draw both. |
| 25% korting on €40 gives €10 as the answer | Found the discount, forgot to subtract | "Is €10 what you pay or what you save?" Ask him to say the answer as a sentence. |
| Percentage increase from 40 to 50 given as 20% | Compared to the new amount (10 out of 50) | "Which was the price at the start? That's your 100%." |
| Freezes at "17%" or "21%" | Unfamiliar number feels impossible | "Build it: 10% + 10% + 1%." Show it's just anchors stuck together. |
| Writes 0,5% for one half | Mixing decimals and percentages | "One half of 100 is…?" Use the money grid (activity 1). |

## "I can…" goals

- I can say ½, ¼, ¾, 1/10, 1/5 and 1/100 as decimals and percentages without looking them up.
- I can turn a fraction, decimal or percentage into the other two.
- I can put a mix of fractions, decimals and percentages in order.
- I can work out a percentage of an amount by finding 10%, 50% or 1% first.
- I can work out a new price after a discount (*korting*), and a price with VAT (*btw*).
- I can say what percentage something went up or down by.
