# 8.7 Activities: ready for the brugklas

[← Unit overview](README.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md)

## 1. Thermometer and lift

**You need:** a strip of paper about 40 cm long, a pen, a counter or coin.

Draw a vertical number line from −10 at the bottom to +10 at the top, with 0 in the middle. Call it a thermometer (or a lift in a building with a car park underneath: floor −1, −2, −3).

Call out moves: "It's 3 degrees. It gets 5 degrees colder." He moves the counter and reads the answer: −2. "The lift is at floor −2 and goes up 6 floors." Floor 4. Then let him call out moves for you.

Look up today's temperature in a cold place (Oslo, Moscow, Antarctica) and a warm one. How many degrees difference?

**Talk about it:** "When did you last see a negative number in real life?" (Freezer, winter weather, a bank balance, below sea level: much of the Netherlands is!)

## 2. Square tiles

**You need:** squared paper, coloured pencils, or Lego/tiles.

Build squares: 1 by 1, 2 by 2, 3 by 3 … up to 10 by 10. Count the tiles each time and write: 1² = 1, 2² = 4, 3² = 9 … Then reverse: "I have 49 tiles in a square. How long is a side?" That's the square root: √49 = 7.

Try a number that doesn't make a square, like 50 tiles. Can you make a perfect square? (No: 7 × 7 = 49 is one short; 8 × 8 = 64 is too many. So √50 is between 7 and 8.)

**Talk about it:** "Why do you think it's called 'squared'?"

## 3. Guess my rule (number machine)

**You need:** paper, a pencil.

One person secretly thinks of a rule, like "× 3, then + 2". The other says an input number; the rule-maker says the output. Fill in a table as you go:

| in | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| out | 5 | 8 | 17 | 32 |

Try to guess the rule from the table. Then write it with a letter: *out = 3 × in + 2*, or *y = 3x + 2*. Start with one-step rules (+ 4, × 5) and build up.

**Talk about it:** "Which input numbers are most useful for guessing the rule?" (0, 1 and 10 are very revealing.)

## 4. Bracket battle

**You need:** dice (2 or 3), paper.

Roll three dice, for example 2, 3 and 5. Each player makes the **biggest** number they can using the three numbers, any of +, −, × and brackets. For example (2 + 3) × 5 = 25, or 2 × 3 × 5 = 30. Then try to make the **smallest** number, or a target number like 11. Explain the order the sum is done in.

**Talk about it:** "How do brackets change the answer? Can you find a sum where brackets make no difference?"

## 5. Brugklas role play

**You need:** two chairs, a "textbook" (any book), the help sentences from the README.

He's the pupil, you're the teacher (then swap). Act out short scenes:

- He doesn't understand an exercise in the lesson and asks for help.
- He forgot his homework. What does he say?
- He got a 4,8 on a test. He goes to ask the teacher what to practise.
- He wants to tell his mentor that maths makes him nervous.

Use the Dutch sentences. Make it a bit funny: an over-strict teacher, a very kind one. The point is that saying the words feels normal.

**Talk about it:** "Which of those would be hardest to do for real? What would make it easier?"

## 6. Visit or look around

**You need:** the secondary school's website or open day (*open dag*).

Look at the school's maths pages, the timetable, the book list. If there's an open day or welcome afternoon (*kennismakingsmiddag*), visit the maths classroom. Seeing the place shrinks the unknown.

**Talk about it:** "What are you most looking forward to? What are you unsure about?"

## Conversation prompts for any day

- "It's −4 °C now and +6 °C this afternoon. How much warmer will it get?"
- "What's 9 squared? And the square root of 81?"
- "If a = 3, what is 2a + 1?"
- "What would you say to a teacher if you didn't understand something?"
- "Who could you go to at your new school if something isn't going well?"
