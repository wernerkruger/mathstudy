# 8.7 Ready for the brugklas

[← Groep 8 overview](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

After the doorstroomtoets, groep 8 relaxes a little, and there's room to look ahead. In September 2029 he starts the first year of secondary school (*brugklas*). The first maths chapters there usually build on primary-school topics, but a few ideas appear that he may never have seen. Meeting them early, in a relaxed way, means the first weeks of the brugklas feel like "I've seen this" instead of "I'm lost".

This unit gives a gentle first look at:

- **Negative numbers** (*negatieve getallen*): below zero on the thermometer, the number line in both directions.
- **Squares and square roots** (*kwadraat en wortel*): 5² = 25 and √25 = 5. LOWAN's groep 8 line includes squaring, powers and roots as orientation.
- **Letters and formulas as "number machines"** (*formules, rekenregels*): a first taste of algebra.
- **Order of operations** (*volgorde van bewerkingen*): why 2 + 3 × 4 is 14 and not 20.

It also covers the practical side: what changes in a brugklas maths lesson, how to ask for help there, and a light **summer bridge plan** for July–August 2029.

**This is orientation, not mastery.** Secondary school will teach all of it properly. The goal is familiarity and confidence, not perfection.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Negative numbers.** Use a thermometer and a lift. Start at 3 °C, the temperature drops 5 degrees: count down past zero to −2 °C. In the brugklas children learn rules for adding and subtracting negatives; for now, the number line is enough.

**Squares and roots.** 5² means 5 × 5 = 25. The picture is a square of 5 by 5 tiles. The square root reverses it: √25 asks "which number times itself makes 25?" Answer: 5. Knowing the squares up to 10² = 100 (and maybe 12² = 144) is plenty.

**Number machines.** Before formulas, use machines: "Put a number in, ×2, then +1." Put in 3, out comes 7. Then turn it into a formula with a letter: *uitvoer = 2 × invoer + 1*, or *y = 2x + 1*. Real-life versions: a taxi costs €4 plus €2 per km; bike hire €10 per day plus €5 deposit. In the brugklas, formulas and tables go together, so practise making a table from a rule.

**Order of operations.** Dutch schools often use the mnemonic **"Hoe Moeten Wij Van De Onvoldoendes Afkomen?"** (*How do we get rid of the fails?*):

1. **H**aakjes (brackets)
2. **M**achtsverheffen (powers) and **W**orteltrekken (roots)
3. **V**ermenigvuldigen and **D**elen (× and :), from left to right
4. **O**ptellen and **A**ftrekken (+ and −), from left to right

So 2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14. With brackets, (2 + 3) × 4 = 20. Ask the brugklas which version they teach; the idea is always the same.

**What to say when he's stuck:** for negatives, "Show me on the thermometer." For squares, "Draw the square." For machines, "Put in an easy number first, like 1 or 10." For order of operations, "Which part do we do first? Put a box around it."

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What is probably going on | What to say or do |
|---|---|---|
| Thinks −8 is bigger than −3 | 8 is bigger than 3 | "Which is colder, −8 °C or −3 °C? Colder is lower." |
| 5² = 10 | Reads ² as × 2 | "Draw it: a square of 5 by 5. How many tiles?" |
| √16 = 8 | Halving instead of rooting | "8 × 8 is…? Which number times itself makes 16?" |
| 2 + 3 × 4 = 20 | Works left to right | "The machine always multiplies first unless there are brackets. Put a box round 3 × 4." |
| Panics at a letter in a sum | Letters look like "real maths" | "The letter is just a box with a number hiding in it. If a = 4, what is 3a?" |

## What changes in the brugklas maths lesson

Share this with him in June, and again in late August. Your school's open evenings and the welcome days will add details.

- **A specialist teacher.** Maths is taught by a maths teacher (*wiskundedocent*), in lessons of usually 45–60 minutes, a few times a week. There is a different teacher for each subject.
- **A textbook and a workbook or online platform.** Each chapter has explanation, worked examples, exercises and a summary. Often there's an online version with extra practice.
- **Homework every lesson or two.** Written in a planner (*agenda*) or shown in the school app. The teacher may check it at the start of a lesson.
- **Announced tests.** Chapter tests (*toetsen, proefwerken*) are announced in advance, so there's always time to prepare. Grades go from 1 to 10, and 5,5 or higher counts as a pass. A single low grade is normal and not a disaster: the average counts.
- **A calculator.** Most schools ask for a specific scientific calculator; it is on the book list (*boekenlijst*). It follows the order of operations (unit 8.5).
- **More independence.** He is expected to ask when he doesn't understand. This is the biggest change, and the one worth practising.

## How to ask for help in the brugklas

Practise these sentences at home, in Dutch, as role play. Saying them out loud a few times makes them much easier to use.

- In the lesson: *"Mevrouw / Meneer, ik snap deze som niet. Kunt u het nog een keer uitleggen?"* ("Miss / Sir, I don't understand this sum. Could you explain it again?")
- After the lesson: *"Mag ik na de les even iets vragen over het huiswerk?"* ("Can I ask something about the homework after the lesson?")
- Specific is easier: *"Ik snap stap 2 niet"* ("I don't understand step 2") is easier for a teacher to help with than "I don't get any of it".
- **The mentor** (*mentor*) is his first contact for anything that isn't going well, including worry about a subject. Parents can contact the mentor too.
- Many schools offer **extra help** (*steunles*, *huiswerkbegeleiding*). Ask at the start of the year, before it is needed.

It may help to tell the mentor at the start of the year, briefly and calmly, that he can get nervous about maths and does best with a clear explanation and a chance to ask questions.

## "I can…" goals

- I can count up and down past zero on a number line or thermometer.
- I can put positive and negative numbers in order.
- I can work out the squares up to 10² and say what a square root is.
- I can use a number machine and write its rule as a formula with a letter.
- I can use the order of operations, including brackets.
- I know what a brugklas maths lesson looks like and how to ask for help.

## Summer bridge plan (July–August 2029)

The goal of the summer is to **arrive rested and confident**, not to get ahead. Plan on 2–3 short sessions a week, mostly games and real-life maths, with a full week off in the middle. Skip any week that's a holiday.

| Week | Focus | Ideas (10–20 minutes, 2–3 times a week) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (early July) | Celebrate | Look through the [progress tracker](../../PROGRESS.md) together. Pick favourite games from [GAMES.md](../../GAMES.md). |
| 2 | Money on holiday | Budget for a day out; prices with korting; percentages in shops (unit 8.1). |
| 3 | Maps and travel | Read a map with a scale; work out travel times and speeds (unit 8.2). |
| 4 | **Week off** | No maths sessions at all. |
| 5 | Kitchen maths | Recipes for more or fewer people; litres and ml (units 8.2, 8.3). |
| 6 | Number machines and negatives | Play "guess my rule"; temperatures in different countries (Sets A and C again). |
| 7 (mid-August) | Squares and brackets | Replay activities 2 and 4. Practice Sets B and D again. |
| 8 (late August) | Ready to go | Check the book list and calculator. Role-play asking for help. Read his "I can" card. |
