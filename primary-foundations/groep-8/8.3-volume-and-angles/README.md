# 8.3 Volume and angles

[← Groep 8 overview](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

Two measuring topics from the domain *Meten en Meetkunde* come together in groep 8:

- **Volume (*inhoud*).** How much fits inside something. Children learn that a cube of 10 × 10 × 10 cm holds exactly 1 litre, so 1 dm³ = 1 litre and 1 cm³ = 1 ml. They work out the volume of a box shape (*balk*) and a cube (*kubus*) as length × width × height.
- **Angles (*hoeken*).** Naming kinds of angles and measuring them in degrees with a protractor (*gradenboog*, often a *geodriehoek* at school).

Both are very hands-on. A kitchen measuring jug, a few boxes and a protractor are all you need. Angles are also a gentle first step into secondary-school geometry.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Volume.** School starts with counting little cubes: a box that is 4 cubes long, 3 wide and 2 high holds 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubes. That leads to the rule *inhoud = lengte × breedte × hoogte*. Then the big link: a 1 dm cube (10 cm × 10 cm × 10 cm = 1 000 cm³) holds exactly 1 litre of water. From that:

| Volume units | Capacity units |
|---|---|
| 1 m³ = 1 000 dm³ | = 1 000 l |
| 1 dm³ | = 1 l = 10 dl = 100 cl = 1 000 ml |
| 1 cm³ | = 1 ml |

Many schools use a "staircase" (*trap*) for the metric system: one step for each unit. For liquid units (l, dl, cl, ml) each step is ×10. For cubic units each step is ×1 000, because a cube grows in three directions. Ask the teacher whether the school uses the staircase, and draw the same one at home.

**Angles.** The school protractor (*geodriehoek*) has two scales running in opposite directions, which is the main source of mistakes. The trick: first decide by eye whether the angle is acute (less than 90°) or obtuse (more than 90°), **then** read the scale. If the angle is clearly acute and the scale says 130°, use the other number (50°).

Useful facts for groep 8: the angles of a triangle add up to 180°; angles on a straight line add up to 180°; a full turn is 360°; the hands of a clock move 30° per hour mark.

**What to say when he's stuck:** for volume, "How many little cubes would fit along the bottom? How many layers?" For angles, "Is it bigger or smaller than the corner of a page?"

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What is probably going on | What to say or do |
|---|---|---|
| Adds length + width + height | Mixing volume with perimeter | Build it with sugar cubes or Lego: "How many in one layer? How many layers?" |
| Thinks 1 m³ = 100 dm³ | Using the ×10 or ×100 step from length/area | Show a 1 m³ space with tape on the floor and broom handles. It holds 10 × 10 × 10 litre-cubes. |
| Reads 130° for an acute angle | Reading the wrong scale on the protractor | "Estimate first: smaller or bigger than a right angle?" |
| Doesn't line up the protractor centre on the corner | Physical handling | Mark the corner with a dot; put the little hole or cross of the protractor exactly on it. |
| Thinks a "longer" angle drawing is a bigger angle | Arm length feels like size | Draw the same angle with short and long arms; measure both. |

## "I can…" goals

- I can say how many ml are in a litre, and that 1 dm³ = 1 litre and 1 cm³ = 1 ml.
- I can work out the volume of a box (*balk*) or cube (*kubus*) as length × width × height.
- I can change cm³ into litres for a box such as an aquarium or a shoebox.
- I can name acute, right, obtuse and straight angles.
- I can measure and draw an angle with a protractor, and check by estimating first.
- I can find a missing angle in a triangle or on a straight line.
