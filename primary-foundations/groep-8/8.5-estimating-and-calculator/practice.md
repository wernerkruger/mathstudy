# 8.5 Practice: estimating and the calculator

[← Unit overview](README.md) · [Answers](answers.md)

These sets start easy and build up. In this unit "about right" often **is** right. There is no clock. You need a calculator only for Sets D and E.

---

## Set A: Rounding (*afronden*) (Warm-up)

Round each number. Picture the number line if it helps: which end is it nearer to?

1. 47 to the nearest ten
2. 382 to the nearest hundred
3. 1 649 to the nearest thousand
4. 3,7 to a whole number
5. 12,49 to a whole number
6. €4,95 to whole euros
7. 2,346 to one decimal
8. 7,85 to one decimal
9. 15 500 to the nearest thousand
10. 0,666 to two decimals
11. 63 to the nearest ten

**Talk about it:** Which ones were exactly halfway? What do we do then?

## Set B: Estimate (*schatten*) (Building)

Don't calculate exactly. Round to friendly numbers and give an estimate. Write down the friendly sum you used.

1. 49 + 31
2. 198 + 403
3. 6 × 49
4. 21 × 19
5. 812 : 4
6. €2,95 + €4,10 + €1,98
7. 3 980 : 20
8. 52 × 11
9. You buy 5 things that each cost €2,99. Is €20 enough?
10. 0,49 × 300

**Talk about it:** For number 9, did you need an exact answer to say yes or no?

## Set C: Does it make sense? (*Klopt het?*) (Building)

A calculator (or a friend in a hurry) gave these answers. Some are right and some are wrong. Use an estimate to decide. If it's wrong, write what it should be about.

1. 38 × 21 = 798
2. 456 + 389 = 7 145
3. 1 200 : 4 = 300
4. 19,95 × 3 = 598,5
5. 2 500 : 50 = 5
6. 72 × 48 = 3 456
7. 9,9 + 0,1 = 10
8. 15% of €80 = €120
9. "The door of our classroom is 2 cm high."
10. "28 children go on a trip in cars that take 4 children each, so we need 7 cars."

**Talk about it:** Which wrong answer was easiest to catch? What gave it away?

## Set D: Head, paper or calculator? (Building → Stretch)

For each sum, first write **H** (head), **P** (paper) or **C** (calculator). Then solve it that way. For the calculator ones, write an estimate first.

1. 100 − 1
2. 25 × 4
3. 3 648 : 12
4. 487 + 235
5. 1,5 × 8
6. 17,8% of €2 345 (round to cents)
7. 999 + 999
8. How many hours are in a year of 365 days?
9. 50% of 846
10. €12,49 × 7

**Talk about it:** Did you and your parent choose the same letter every time? Whose reason was best for number 8?

## Set E: Reading the calculator (Stretch → finish on a win)

Use a calculator. Then decide what the answer **means**.

1. 250 children go on a trip in minibuses with 8 seats each. The calculator says 250 : 8 = 31.25. How many minibuses do you need?
2. Four friends share a restaurant bill of €47,60 equally. The calculator shows 11.9. How much does each friend pay? Write it as money.
3. Type 1 : 3. What does the screen show? Round it to two decimals.
4. Three friends share €20. How much is that each, rounded to cents? What's the problem?
5. Type 3 + 4 × 2. What does your calculator show? What is the correct answer when you multiply first (as in secondary school)?
6. Apples cost €3,98 per kilo. You buy 2,5 kg. What do you pay? (Round to cents.)
7. 365 days : 7 = ? How many **full** weeks are there in a year?
8. 100 eggs are packed in boxes of 8. How many boxes do you need? How many boxes are **completely** full?
9. Type 10 : 2. What does it show?

**Talk about it:** In numbers 1 and 8, why is the calculator's answer not the final answer?

## Puzzle corner (optional, just for fun)

1. Without a calculator: is 99 × 99 more or less than 10 000? By how much? (Hint: 100 × 99 is easy.)
2. Estimate: how many times does your heart beat in a day? (Count your pulse for 15 seconds first, sitting still.)
3. Type 12345679 × 9 into a calculator. Then try 12345679 × 18 and × 27. What's going on?
