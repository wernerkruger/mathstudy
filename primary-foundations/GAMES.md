# Games

[← Back to the track](README.md)

Games build fluency without the pressure of a worksheet. Research on parents' maths anxiety specifically recommends games as a positive way to do maths together (see the [parent guide](PARENT_GUIDE.md)). They count as a full session.

**Rules for every game:**

- No timers. If a game normally has a speed element, play it without one.
- Thinking out loud is allowed and encouraged. So is using paper, fingers or a calculator to check.
- Let him win sometimes, but not every time. Children notice when you lose on purpose. Many of these games involve luck, which evens things out naturally.
- Adults explain their own thinking too: "I've got 6 and 8... I'll do double 6 is 12, plus 2 more is 14."
- If a game causes stress, change the rules to make it easier or more co-operative.

**What you need:** a pack of cards (take out the picture cards unless stated; Ace = 1), 2–4 ordinary dice, paper, pencils, some coins. A 10-sided die or a 0–9 spinner is useful but not essential.

## Numbers and place value

### 1. Biggest number
**Skills:** place value, reading big numbers. **Groep:** 6–7.

Use cards 0–9 (Ace = 1; use a picture card as 0). Each player draws a grid of five boxes: `[ ][ ][ ][ ][ ]`. Turn over one card at a time; everyone writes that digit in one of their boxes. Once written, it can't move. After five cards, read your numbers out loud. Biggest number wins.

*Variations:* smallest number wins; closest to 50,000 wins; add a decimal point for groep 7.

*Talk:* "Why did you put the 9 there? Would it be better at the start?"

### 2. Close to 1000
**Skills:** adding, estimating. **Groep:** 6–7.

Deal six cards each. Make two three-digit numbers whose sum is as close to 1000 as possible. Score = how far you are from 1000. Lowest total after five rounds wins.

### 3. Number line guess
**Skills:** estimating, the number line. **Groep:** 6–8.

Draw a line, mark 0 at one end and 1000 (or 10,000, or 1) at the other. One person names a number; the other marks where it goes. Measure with a ruler to see how close you were. Swap.

### 4. Rounding race (no clock)
**Skills:** rounding. **Groep:** 6.

Draw four cards to make a four-digit number. Each player rounds it to the nearest 10, 100 or 1000 (roll a die: 1–2 = tens, 3–4 = hundreds, 5–6 = thousands). Say it out loud and explain. A point for each correct rounding. No racing; take turns.

## Times tables and division

### 5. Times-table snap (slow)
**Skills:** times tables. **Groep:** 6.

Normal snap is about speed. This version isn't. Turn over two cards and take turns: whoever's turn it is says the product of the two cards and how they worked it out. If correct, they keep both cards. Start with only cards 1–5 and 10; add 6–9 over time.

### 6. Multiplication bingo
**Skills:** times tables. **Groep:** 6.

Each player draws a 3×3 grid and writes nine numbers from the answers of a table (e.g. the 6 times table: 6, 12, 18, …, 60). Roll a die (or 10-sided die); multiply the roll by 6. If you have the answer, cross it out. First line wins.

### 7. Array capture
**Skills:** times tables, area. **Groep:** 6–7.

On squared paper (e.g. a 20×20 grid), take turns rolling two dice and drawing a rectangle that size (a 4 and a 5 means a 4-by-5 rectangle). Write the product inside. If it doesn't fit, you miss a turn. When the grid is full, add up your areas.

### 8. Target 100
**Skills:** mental adding and subtracting. **Groep:** 6.

Start at 0. Take turns rolling two dice and making a two-digit number (a 3 and a 5 can be 35 or 53). Add it to your total. You may also choose to subtract. Aim to land as close to 100 as possible after five turns.

### 9. Remainder race
**Skills:** division with remainder. **Groep:** 6–7.

Draw a track numbered 0–50. Both players start at 50. On your turn, roll a die and divide your current number by the roll. The remainder (*rest*) is how many steps you move back toward 0. First to 0 wins. ("I'm on 38 and rolled a 5. 38 divided by 5 is 7 rest 3. I move back 3.")

### 10. Make it true
**Skills:** all four operations. **Groep:** 6–8.

Draw four cards. Using any of +, −, ×, ÷ and brackets, make the target number (roll two dice for a target, or pick 24). Work together or take turns. There's often more than one way, which is the whole point.

## Fractions and decimals

### 11. Fraction war
**Skills:** comparing fractions. **Groep:** 6–7.

Each player turns over two cards and makes a fraction (*breuk*) with the smaller number on top. Who has the bigger fraction? Draw it or use strips if unsure. Start with fractions with the same denominator (*noemer*) or the same numerator (*teller*).

### 12. Fraction strips race
**Skills:** fractions as parts of a whole. **Groep:** 6.

Cut paper strips the same length and fold them into halves, quarters, eighths, thirds and sixths. Label each piece. Roll a die with faces labelled ½, ¼, ¼, ⅛, ⅛, ⅛ (stickers on a normal die). Collect pieces to fill one whole strip exactly. You can't go over.

### 13. Decimal shop
**Skills:** decimals, money. **Groep:** 6–7.

Price items in the house (€1.25, €0.80, €2.05…). One person is the shopkeeper. Pay with real coins and give change. Ask: "Which costs more, €1.50 or €1.05?"

## Measuring, time and money

### 14. Supermarket estimate
**Skills:** estimating, money. **Groep:** 6–8.

At the supermarket or with a receipt: round each price to the nearest euro and estimate the total before you get to the till. How close were you? (He can be the "estimator" while you shop.)

### 15. Kitchen scales guess
**Skills:** weight, estimating. **Groep:** 6–7.

Pick five things from the kitchen. Guess each weight in grams, then weigh. Points for the closest guess. Try putting them in order first.

### 16. How long is a minute?
**Skills:** time, estimating. **Groep:** 6.

Close your eyes and say "now" when you think a minute has passed. Someone checks with a clock. This is the only game with a clock, and it is used to check a guess, not to rush him.

### 17. Train timetable
**Skills:** durations, reading tables. **Groep:** 6–8.

Plan a real or pretend trip using the NS app or a printed timetable. "The train leaves at 10:47 and arrives at 11:32. How long is the journey?" Plan a day out: when do we need to leave home?

## Shape, space and graphs

### 18. Map treasure hunt
**Skills:** coordinates, scale. **Groep:** 6–7.

Draw a grid over a map of your town or a holiday map. Hide "treasure" in a square (e.g. C4); give clues using coordinates and distances. Use the scale bar to say how far away it is.

### 19. Battleships
**Skills:** coordinates. **Groep:** 6–7.

The classic game on a 10×10 grid with letters along one side and numbers along the other.

### 20. Build what I see
**Skills:** views (front, side, top), spatial thinking. **Groep:** 6–8.

Build a small model from 5–8 blocks or Lego bricks. Draw it from the front, side and top. The other person has to build it from the drawings only.

### 21. Which one doesn't belong?
**Skills:** reasoning, talking maths. **Groep:** 6–8.

Write four numbers or draw four shapes, e.g. 9, 16, 25, 43. Ask "Which one doesn't belong?" Every answer is fine if it comes with a reason (43 isn't a square number; 16 is the only even one; 9 is the only one-digit number).

### 22. Ratio table relay
**Skills:** ratio tables. **Groep:** 6–8.

Take a recipe for 4 people. Build a ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*) together to scale it for 2, 6, 8 or 10 people. Then cook it.

## Classics

### 23. Yahtzee
**Skills:** adding, multiplying, chance. **Groep:** 6–8.

The full scoring sheet is excellent mental arithmetic. Let him keep score.

### 24. Shut the box (paper version)
**Skills:** number bonds, adding. **Groep:** 6.

Write the numbers 1–9 in a row. Roll two dice and add them; cross out any numbers that add up to that total (a total of 8: cross out 8, or 5 and 3, or 1, 3 and 4). Keep going until you can't. Your score is the sum of the numbers left. Lowest score wins.
