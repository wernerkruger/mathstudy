# 7.3 Fractions

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

In groep 6 fractions (*breuken*) were mostly "part of a whole": 3/4 of a pizza, 1/2 of 20. In groep 7 they become numbers you calculate with: making equal fractions (*gelijknamig maken*), simplifying (*vereenvoudigen*), adding and subtracting (also with different denominators), mixed numbers (*gemengde getallen*), a fraction times a whole number, and a first look at a fraction times a fraction. The new national goals stress seeing a fraction as a number on the number line, as well as a part of something and a division (3/4 is also 3 : 4). Fractions are the topic most adults remember as confusing, so go slowly and keep pictures on the table the whole time.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Models school uses.** Fraction strips (*breukenstroken*), circles ("pizzas"), rectangles (bars of chocolate) and the number line. Most Dutch methods also use the ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*) for equivalent fractions: a table with 3 on top and 4 underneath, then ×2 gives 6 and 8. Keep pictures available even when he seems fine; it's not "babyish", it's how mathematicians think too.

**Equivalent fractions and simplifying.** Multiply (or divide) the top and the bottom by the same number: 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12. Simplifying (*vereenvoudigen*) is the same thing backwards: 6/8 → divide both by 2 → 3/4. School says "as simple as possible" (*zo eenvoudig mogelijk*). Avoid the phrase "cancel"; school doesn't use it.

**Adding and subtracting.** With the same denominator (*gelijknamig*), add the tops only: 3/8 + 4/8 = 7/8. **The denominator stays the same**, because eighths plus eighths are still eighths. With different denominators (*ongelijknamig*), first make them the same: 1/2 + 1/3 → 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. Strips make this visible: you can't add halves and thirds until you cut them into the same size pieces.

**Mixed numbers.** 2 1/2 is two wholes and a half. School converts both ways: 11/3 = 3 2/3 ("how many whole threes fit in 11?"), and 3 1/4 = 13/4. When adding mixed numbers, most methods add the wholes and the fractions separately.

**Fraction × whole number.** 3 × 2/5 means three lots of 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5. And "3/4 of 20" is the same as 3/4 × 20: find 1/4 of 20 (5), then three of those (15).

**Fraction × fraction.** Introduced as "of": 1/2 × 3/4 is "half of 3/4" = 3/8. The rectangle model shows it best: shade 3/4 of a rectangle one way, then half of that the other way. In groep 7 the picture is more important than the "top × top, bottom × bottom" rule; let the rule come from the pictures.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 | Very common, and not silly: he's adding tops and bottoms. "Let's draw it. Is 2/5 more or less than 1/2? Can adding something to 1/2 make it smaller?" Then use strips. |
| 3/8 + 4/8 = 7/16 | "What size are the pieces? Eighths. After adding, what size are they?" |
| Thinks 1/8 is bigger than 1/6 because 8 > 6 | "Would you rather share a pizza with 6 people or with 8?" |
| Doesn't know when to stop simplifying | "Can the top and bottom both still be divided by the same number?" A times-table chart helps. |
| Freezes at mixed numbers | Use real things: 2 1/2 apples, 3 3/4 pizzas. Draw the wholes as circles. |
| "Times makes bigger" confusion: 1/2 × 3/4 gives something smaller | "Half of something is smaller than the thing. 'Times a fraction' means 'a part of'." |

## "I can…" goals

- I can find fractions that are equal (like 3/4 = 6/8) and show it with a drawing.
- I can make a fraction as simple as possible.
- I can add and subtract fractions with the same denominator.
- I can make fractions gelijknamig and then add or subtract them.
- I can change a fraction like 11/4 into a mixed number and back.
- I can work out 3/4 of a number, and a fraction times a whole number.
- I can explain with a drawing why 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.
