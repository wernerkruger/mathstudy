# Groep 7 (school year 2027–28)

[← Back to the track](../README.md) · [Parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) · [Resources](../RESOURCES.md) · [Progress tracker](../PROGRESS.md) · [Groep 6](../groep-6/)

Groep 7 is the year when primary maths gets "bigger": numbers up to a million, long division (*staartdeling*), fractions (*breuken*) that need the same denominator before you add them, percentages (*procenten*) and area (*oppervlakte*). It is also the year many children first decide they are "bad at maths". The aim here is the opposite: he arrives in groep 8 knowing he can do this, because he has practised each topic calmly, a little ahead of school.

Nothing here is new in principle. Every groep 7 topic grows out of something from groep 6. When something feels hard, go back one step to what he already knows and build up from there.

## How this year maps to school

The order below follows the LOWAN learning line for groep 7–8 ([LOWAN leerlijn rekenen groep 7–8, PDF](https://www.lowan.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Groep-7-8-Leerlijn-Rekenen-MRT26-PRINT.pdf)), which splits the year into "begin groep 7" and "midden groep 7". Methods such as *De wereld in getallen* follow a similar sequence, but the exact weeks differ. Ask the teacher at the start of the year which method and chapter they are on, and move units to stay a week or two ahead of class.

Notes on the table:

- **The four domains.** Dutch pupil-tracking tests (LVS) and the referentieniveaus report maths in four domains: Numbers (*Getallen*), Ratios (*Verhoudingen*), Measurement and Geometry (*Meten en Meetkunde*) and Relationships (*Verbanden*). Decimals sit between Numbers and Ratios; schools file them under either.
- **Metric system and area come early in LOWAN** (begin groep 7) but last here, because they lean on decimals and multiplying. If school does them in the autumn, do unit 7.7 then. It stands on its own: the only things it needs are × and ÷ by 10/100/1000 (unit 7.1) and simple decimals from groep 6.
- **The new national goals** (*kerndoelen rekenen-wiskunde*, law since August 2026) add more reasoning, estimating, choosing how to calculate, and reading graphs critically (*kritisch denken*). Each unit has "talk about it" prompts for this, and unit 7.8 has a section on graphs that are drawn to persuade. Schools may switch to the new goals at any time until August 2031, so his school may or may not stress this yet.
- **Mid-year and end-of-year tests.** Most schools give LVS tests (often Cito) around January–February and May–June; I'm assuming the same pattern as groep 6, so check the dates with the teacher. The [parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md) has advice on keeping these calm. The official provisional secondary-school advice (*voorlopig advies*) comes in groep 8, around January 2029. Many schools probably also share a first indication (often called *pre-advies*) at the end of groep 7; ask whether his school does.

## Notation used in the practice sheets

Child-facing sheets use Dutch school notation, so they look like his schoolwork:

- decimal comma: 3,5 (in English text: 3.5)
- a space between thousands: 250 000 (school may write 250.000; a full stop there is *not* a decimal point)
- division with a colon: 84 : 4 = 21
- money: € 3,75

## Each unit has

- `README.md`: what and why, Dutch terms, "Parent in 2 minutes", stumbling blocks and what to say, "I can…" goals, a 3–5-week plan, links.

- `practice.md`: short sets labelled **Warm-up**, **Building** and **Stretch**. Every set starts with items he will get right and ends on an easy one, so he always finishes on a success.
- `answers.md`: answers with a one-line strategy each.

A typical session is 15–20 minutes, 4–5 days a week (see "Session structure" in the [parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md)). Do not try to do a whole practice set in one sitting if it is going slowly; half a set and a game is a good session.

## Start-of-year check-in (September 2027)

A relaxed look at groep 6 skills before you start unit 7.1. It is **not a test**. Do it over two or three sessions, a few items at a time, at the kitchen table with a drink. He can use scrap paper and talk out loud. If something is shaky, that is useful information, not a problem: spend a week on the matching [groep 6](../groep-6/) unit before moving on.

How to introduce it: *"New school year, new notebook. Let's have a look at what you already know from last year. Lots of this will feel easy."*

### Part A: Numbers and calculating

1. Write in digits: *zesenveertigduizend zevenhonderdvijftig* (forty-six thousand, seven hundred and fifty).
2. Round that number to the nearest thousand (*afronden op duizendtallen*).
3. 30 × 40 =
4. 487 + 235 =
5. 1 705 − 346 =
6. 4 × 536 =
7. 92 : 4 =
8. 826 : 9 = … rest …

### Part B: Fractions, decimals, ratio

9. What is 3/4 of 24?
10. Which is bigger: 0,4 or 0,35? How do you know?
11. Fill in the ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*):

| Packs of pens | 4 | 2 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pens | 6 | ? | ? |

### Part C: Measuring and time

12. A rectangle is 7 cm long and 4 cm wide. What is its perimeter (*omtrek*)?
13. The film starts at 15:40 and you arrive 25 minutes later. What time is it?

### How did it go?

Ask, rather than mark: *"Which ones felt easy? Which one would you like to look at again?"* Tick the ones he got right. For the others, look at how he worked, not just the answer.

| If this was shaky | Look again at |
|---|---|
| 1–2 (big numbers, rounding) | groep 6 numbers to 100,000, then go slowly in 7.1 |
| 4–6 (column methods) | groep 6 adding/subtracting and multiplying units before 7.2 |
| 7–8 (dividing) | groep 6 multiplying and dividing; play "Remainder race" in [GAMES.md](../GAMES.md) |
| 3, 6–8 and slow tables | times tables through games, a little every day, alongside 7.1 |
| 9–11 | groep 6 fractions, decimals and ratio tables |
| 12–13 | groep 6 measuring, time and money |
