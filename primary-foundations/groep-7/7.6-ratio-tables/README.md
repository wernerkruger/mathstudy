# 7.6 Ratio tables (*verhoudingstabel*)

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

The ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*) is the Swiss army knife of Dutch primary maths. It's a two-row table that keeps two amounts "in step": people and grams of flour, kilos and euros, hours and kilometres. He met it in groep 6; in groep 7 he uses it for recipes, prices and speed, and in groep 8 for scale, percentages and almost every word problem on the doorstroomtoets. A child who is at home with ratio tables has a calm, reliable way into most word problems.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**What it looks like.** Two rows, each with a label, and columns that stay in proportion:

| Persons | 4 | 8 | 2 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flour (g) | 250 | 500 | 125 | 375 |

**The moves school teaches.** Whatever you do to the top, do the same to the bottom:
- double (× 2) or halve (: 2)
- × 10 or : 10
- add two columns (2 persons + 4 persons = 6 persons, so 125 g + 250 g = 375 g)
- subtract two columns
- go back to 1 (*terugrekenen naar 1*), then up again

There is no single "right" route. Children are expected to choose sensible steps, and they get credit for clear steps. Let him pick; ask him to explain.

**Speed.** 15 km per hour means the table 1 hour → 15 km. Then halve for half an hour, × 3 for three hours, and so on. Minutes are tricky because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. Use friendly chunks: 30, 15, 20, 10 minutes.

**Better buy.** Comparing prices usually means making one row the same in both tables (e.g. price per 100 g, or per kilo).

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Adds the same amount to both rows (4 → 6 people, so 250 → 252 g) | "We went from 4 to 6 people. Did we add 2 people, or did we make it one and a half times as much? What happens to the flour if we have 2 more *people*?" Use the add-two-columns move. |
| Gets lost in a long table | "Write the step on top of the arrow: ×2, :4." Arrows make the thinking visible. |
| Treats minutes like decimals (1,5 hours = 1 hour 50 minutes) | "Half an hour is how many minutes?" Use a clock face. |
| Picks awkward steps and gets stuck with messy numbers | "Is there a friendlier stop on the way? Can we go to 1 first? To 10?" |
| Labels missing, mixes up rows | "What does the top row mean? And the bottom one?" Always write labels. |

## "I can…" goals

- I can fill in a ratio table by doubling, halving, × 10 and : 10.
- I can add two columns together to find a new one.
- I can change a recipe for more or fewer people.
- I can work out prices with a ratio table and find the better buy.
- I can work out distances and times when I know the speed.
- I can explain which steps I chose and why.
