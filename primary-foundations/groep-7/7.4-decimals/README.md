# 7.4 Decimals (*kommagetallen*)

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

In groep 6 he met tenths and hundredths on the number line. In groep 7 decimals (*kommagetallen*) become everyday tools: putting them in order, adding and subtracting them, multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1000, and rounding money. Decimals are everywhere in real life (prices, kilometres, kilos, race results) and they connect fractions (unit 7.3) to percentages (unit 7.5), so time spent here pays off twice.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Commas, not points.** At school 3,75 is "drie komma vijfenzeventig". In English it's 3.75. Tell him they're the same and that you'll write it the school way in his practice. Money is the best model he has: € 3,75 is 3 euros and 75 cents, and 75 cents is 75 hundredths of a euro.

**Place value continues past the comma.** E | t | h | d (*eenheden, tienden, honderdsten, duizendsten*). School often uses a place-value table with a fat line for the comma, and number lines "zoomed in": between 2 and 3 there are 10 tenths, between 2,4 and 2,5 there are 10 hundredths.

**Ordering.** The trick schools teach is to make the numbers equally long by adding zeros at the end: 0,3 → 0,30, then compare 0,30 with 0,25. Adding zeros at the end of a decimal doesn't change it (0,3 = 0,30 = 0,300); adding zeros in the middle does (0,3 ≠ 0,03).

**× and ÷ by 10, 100, 1000.** Exactly as in unit 7.1: every digit moves one place left (× 10) or right (: 10). The comma stays where it is and the digits move; some methods instead say "the comma jumps", which gives the same answer. Use whichever words he hears at school. Don't say "add a zero".

**Rounding money.** Two kinds: rounding to whole euros for estimating (€ 3,47 is about € 3), and the cash rule in Dutch shops: when you pay cash, most shops round the total to the nearest 5 cents (€ 3,47 → € 3,45; € 3,48 → € 3,50). The 1- and 2-cent coins are rarely used. Card payments stay exact.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Thinks 0,25 is bigger than 0,3 "because 25 is bigger than 3" | Very common. "Let's write them as money: € 0,25 and € 0,30. Which would you rather have?" Then fill the zeros: 0,25 and 0,30. |
| Thinks 3,08 and 3,8 are the same | "Is 8 cents the same as 80 cents?" Use a ruler: 3,08 m and 3,8 m. |
| 2,5 × 10 = 2,50 | "Did it get ten times bigger? 2,50 is the same as 2,5. Let's put it in the place-value table and move the digits." |
| Lines up decimals by the end when adding: 5,6 + 2,75 → 3,31 | *Komma onder komma*. Write 5,60. |
| Reads 0,07 as "seven tenths" | Say it the long way: "zero comma zero seven, that's 7 hundredths, 7 cents." |

## "I can…" goals

- I can say what each digit in a kommagetal is worth (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).
- I can put kommagetallen in order, also when they have different numbers of digits.
- I can add and subtract kommagetallen.
- I can multiply and divide kommagetallen by 10, 100 and 1000.
- I can round kommagetallen to whole numbers or one decimal place.
- I can round money to whole euros and to 5 cents, like at the till.
