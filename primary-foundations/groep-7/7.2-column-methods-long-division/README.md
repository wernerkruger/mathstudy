# 7.2 Column methods and long division

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

This unit is about calculating on paper when the numbers are too big for your head: adding and subtracting in columns, now also with decimals (*kommagetallen*); multiplying by a two-digit number; and long division (*staartdeling*), first with "happy" numbers that divide exactly, then with a remainder (*rest*) or a decimal answer. These written methods are what the groep 8 tests and secondary school expect him to be able to do reliably, and once they're secure they take a lot of worry away: there is always a way to get the answer.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**First, ask the teacher which division method the school uses.** This matters more than anything else in this unit. Many Dutch schools teach **happen** ("taking bites"): to do 672 : 6 you take big friendly bites, "100 × 6 = 600, that leaves 72; 10 × 6 = 60, leaves 12; 2 × 6 = 12, leaves 0", then add the bites: 100 + 10 + 2 = 112. Other schools (and *De wereld in getallen* in later chapters) teach the **traditional staartdeling**, written with the divisor on the left and the answer built up digit by digit. Some do happen first and staartdeling later. **Use only the method school uses.** If you learned a different way (for example the English "bus stop" layout), don't show it; two methods at once is a common source of confusion and tears. The activities and answers here work for both.

**Column adding and subtracting.** He already knows this from groep 6 (487 + 235, 1705 − 346). The new part is decimals: write the numbers so the commas are underneath each other (*komma onder komma*), fill gaps with zeros if it helps (3,7 becomes 3,70), and calculate as usual. The comma in the answer goes straight under the others.

**Long multiplication.** For 46 × 23 school writes 46 on top, 23 below, then: 3 × 46 = 138 on the first line, 20 × 46 = 920 on the second line, and adds them: 1 058. Some schools write the 0 for the 20 explicitly, others leave a gap. Follow his exercise book.

**Remainders.** 826 : 9 = 91 rest 7. In a word problem the remainder needs thought: 130 children, 50 per bus, means 3 buses (round up), not "2 rest 30". Sharing € 30 among 4 people gives € 7,50 each (keep dividing to get a decimal). Groep 7 asks him to decide which one fits the story.

**Estimate first, check after.** The new national goals stress this. Before 23 × 46: "about 20 × 50 = 1 000". Afterwards: "Is 1 058 close? Yes." Make this a habit; it also gives him a feeling of control.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Lines up decimals by the right-hand edge: 12,45 + 3,7 → 12,82 | "Where's the comma? Let's put the commas under each other, and write 3,70 so both have two digits after the comma." Use money: € 12,45 + € 3,70. |
| 5 000 − 1 234 goes wrong with all the zeros | Do it with the method from school. As a check: "1 234 + what makes 5 000?" Counting up is fine as a check. |
| Forgets the second line in 23 × 46 (only does 3 × 46) | "23 is 20 and 3. You've done the 3 lots. Where are the 20 lots?" |
| Staartdeling feels overwhelming | Go back to happy numbers (Practice set 4) and to big friendly bites: "Can you take 100 sixes? 10 sixes?" Fewer, bigger bites are fine; smaller bites are also fine. |
| Panics at a remainder | "A remainder isn't a mistake. It means the sharing doesn't come out exactly, like 7 sweets for 2 people." |
| Writes "3 buses rest 30" style answers | "Read the question again. What do they want to know? Can 30 children stay home?" |

## "I can…" goals

- I can add and subtract big numbers in columns.
- I can add and subtract kommagetallen by putting the commas under each other.
- I can multiply a number by a two-digit number on paper.
- I can divide on paper, first with numbers that come out exactly.
- I can divide when there is a remainder, and decide what to do with it in a story.
- I can estimate first and check my answer afterwards.

| 3 | Long multiplication | Practice set 3, Activity 3 (Area model on squared paper) |
| 4 | Division with happy numbers | Practice set 4, Activity 4 (Sharing the treasure) |
| 5 | Remainders and decimal answers; word problems | Practice set 5, Activity 5 (What do we do with the rest?), "Remainder race" in [GAMES.md](../../GAMES.md) |

Long division is often the topic that worries children most in groep 7. If it doesn't click, stay on happy numbers for an extra week. It is much better to be secure on exact divisions than shaky on everything.
