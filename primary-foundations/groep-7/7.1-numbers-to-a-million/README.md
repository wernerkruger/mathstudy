# 7.1 Numbers to a million

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

In groep 6 he worked with numbers up to 100,000. Now the number system grows to 1,000,000 (*een miljoen*): reading and writing big numbers, knowing what each digit is worth, rounding them sensibly, and multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1000. This is the foundation for everything else this year, especially decimals (unit 7.4) and the metric system (unit 7.7), which use exactly the same "shift one place" idea.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**Place-value table.** School uses a table with columns HD | TD | D | H | T | E (sometimes with M for *miljoen* in front). Each digit sits in a column. "What is the 4 worth in 346 512?" means: which column is it in? It's in the TD column, so 40,000.

**Writing big numbers.** School writes 346 512 with a space or 346.512 with a full stop. Both are fine. Never use a comma: in Dutch, the comma is the decimal point. Dutch number words are written as one long word (*driehonderdzesenveertigduizend vijfhonderdtwaalf*); saying them out loud in chunks ("three hundred forty-six thousand … five hundred twelve") is the trick, and it's the same in both languages.

**Number line.** School uses empty and partly-labelled number lines, e.g. from 0 to 1,000,000 with marks every 100,000. He'll be asked where a number goes, or what number an arrow points at. Ask "What's halfway?" a lot; it's the key skill.

**Rounding (*afronden*).** Look at the digit just to the right of the place you're rounding to. 0–4: round down. 5–9: round up. Many teachers also say "which is it closer to on the number line?", which is the more understandable version. Use the number line first, and the rule second.

**× and ÷ by 10, 100, 1000.** School says the digits shift one place left for each zero (× 10: one place, × 1000: three places), and shift right for dividing. **Avoid "just add a zero"**: it works for whole numbers but breaks with decimals in a few months (2,5 × 10 is 25, not 2,50). Say "every digit moves one place to the left, and the empty place gets a 0".

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Writes "three hundred and six thousand two hundred" as 3 006 200 or 30 6200 | "Let's put it in the place-value table. Thousands first: 306. Then the rest: 200, which needs three places: 200." Each block of three digits is a "group". |
| Thinks 98 765 is bigger than 120 000 because it "has bigger digits" | "Count the digits first. Which one has more places?" Then show both on a number line. |
| Rounds 61 499 to 62 000 (looks at all the 9s) | "Which thousand is it between, 61 000 or 62 000? Which is it closer to?" Only one digit decides. |
| Unsure about 4 500 → 5 000 | "It's exactly halfway. The rule is: halfway goes up." Agree it's just a rule everyone uses. |
| Panic at long numbers | Cover the number with a hand and reveal one group of three at a time. Big numbers are just small numbers with a "thousand" label. |

## "I can…" goals

- I can read and write numbers up to a million.
- I can say what each digit in a big number is worth.
- I can put big numbers in order and place them on a number line.
- I can round big numbers to the nearest thousand, ten thousand or hundred thousand.
- I can multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000 and explain what happens to the digits.
- I can explain to someone else when rounding is useful.
