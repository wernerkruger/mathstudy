# 7.7 Answers: metric system and area

[← Unit 7.7](README.md) · [Practice](practice.md)

Check the units as well as the numbers: perimeter in cm or m, area in cm² or m². A right number with a missing unit is still a right number; just mention the unit.

## Set 1 · Warm-up: lengths

1. 100 cm
2. 1 000 m
3. 10 mm
4. 300 cm
5. 2 000 m
6. 5 cm (one step up: : 10)
7. 4 m (two steps up: : 100)
8. 10 dm
9. 50 cm

Talk about it: smaller. Metres are bigger than centimetres, so you need fewer of them.

## Set 5 · Stretch: composite shapes and m²/cm²

1. 20 m² (6 × 4 = 24, minus the bite 2 × 2 = 4)
2. 20 m (6 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 4 + 4; the same as the full rectangle)
3. 23 m² (15 + 8)
4. 74 m² (80 − 6)
5. 184 cm² (600 − 416)
6. 10 000 cm² (100 × 100)
7. 100 tiles (10 along each side)
8. 24 tiles (4 tiles per m², 6 m²; or 6 tiles along by 4 tiles across)
9. 6 m²

Talk about it: no. The two edges of the "bite" (2 m + 2 m) replace the two pieces of edge that were cut away (2 m + 2 m), so the walk around is just as long.

## Puzzle corner

- With 12 m of fence: 1 × 5 (5 m²), 2 × 4 (8 m²), 3 × 3 (9 m²). The square, 3 × 3, gives the most space.
- With 20 m: 5 × 5 = 25 m² is best. The closer to a square, the more space.
- Against a wall (fence on three sides only): 3 m out from the wall and 6 m along it gives 18 m², the best with whole metres. Now the best shape is *not* a square; nice to discover.
