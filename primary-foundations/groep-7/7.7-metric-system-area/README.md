# 7.7 Metric system and area

[← Groep 7](../README.md) · [Activities](activities.md) · [Practice](practice.md) · [Answers](answers.md) · [Parent guide](../../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Games](../../GAMES.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## What and why

The metric system (*metriek stelsel*) is the "ladder" of units for length, weight and capacity: km, m, cm, mm; kg, g; l, ml, and the steps in between. In groep 7 he learns to move up and down that ladder, also with decimals (1,5 m = 150 cm), and he works with perimeter (*omtrek*: the distance around a shape) and area (*oppervlakte*: how much space is inside it), in cm² and m², including shapes made of more than one rectangle. This is very practical maths: furniture, gardens, floors, maps, cooking.

**Timing note:** LOWAN places this at the *start* of groep 7. If school does it in the autumn, do this unit then. It needs only × and : by 10/100/1000 (unit 7.1) and simple decimals from groep 6.

## Parent in 2 minutes

**The ladder.** School draws a staircase or ladder: km – hm – dam – m – dm – cm – mm. The memory sentence is *"Kan Het Dametje Met De Centimeter Meten?"* ("Can the little lady measure with the centimetre?"), where the first letters give k, h, d(a), m, d, c, m. For length, each step down is × 10 and each step up is : 10. So 1 m = 10 dm = 100 cm = 1 000 mm, and 1 km = 1 000 m. The same ladder works for litres (kl … l, dl, cl, ml) and grams (kg, hg, dag, g …), though in daily life only some rungs are used. Dutch shops still say *een ons* for 100 g.

**Converting with commas.** Moving one rung on the ladder = × or : 10, which is exactly the "digits shift" from units 7.1 and 7.4. 1,5 m → cm is two rungs down, so × 100 = 150 cm. Many schools use a place-value table with the units as column headings (km | hm | dam | m | dm | cm | mm) and write one digit per column; this is a good visual if he gets lost.

**Perimeter (*omtrek*).** Walk around the edge and add all sides. For a rectangle: length + width + length + width. School doesn't usually teach a formula like 2 × (l + w) yet; adding the four sides is fine.

**Area (*oppervlakte*).** Count squares first (on squared paper), then notice that a rectangle is rows × columns, so length × width. Units are squares: cm² (a 1 cm by 1 cm square), m² (a 1 m by 1 m square, about the size of a large tablecloth). For composite (L-shaped) shapes, split into rectangles or "take a bite out of" a bigger rectangle.

**m² and cm²:** 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm², not 100 cm². This surprises everybody, adults included. Make a real 1 m² from newspaper and let him see how many 10 × 10 cm tiles fit. In groep 7 this is a Stretch topic; converting area units properly comes in groep 8.

## Common stumbling blocks and what to say

| What you might see | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Multiplies when going up the ladder | "Is a kilometre bigger or smaller than a metre? So will the number of kilometres be bigger or smaller?" Big unit → small number. |
| Unsure how many steps between units | Point at the ladder and count rungs. Write the sentence on a card and keep it on the table. |
| Mixes up omtrek and oppervlakte | Omtrek: "an ant walks around the edge" (a length, in m). Oppervlakte: "paint the inside" (squares, in m²). |
| Writes area in m instead of m² | "Are we measuring a line or a surface? A surface is counted in squares." |
| Composite shape: counts a side twice or misses one | Colour each rectangle; or, for perimeter, put a finger on a corner and walk round. |
| Thinks 1 m² = 100 cm² | Build it (Activity 4). |

## "I can…" goals

- I can use the metric ladder and say *Kan Het Dametje Met De Centimeter Meten*.
- I can convert between km, m, cm and mm, also with kommagetallen.
- I can convert between litres and ml, kg and g.
- I can work out the perimeter (omtrek) of a rectangle and other shapes.
- I can work out the area (oppervlakte) of a rectangle in cm² or m².
- I can find the area of an L-shape by splitting it or taking a bite out.
- I can explain the difference between omtrek and oppervlakte.
