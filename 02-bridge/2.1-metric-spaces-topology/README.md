# 2.1 Metric Spaces and Topology

**Phase:** 2 (Bridge)
**Estimated length:** 8 weeks at ~6 hours/week (about 48 hours)

## Prerequisites

- [1.1 Proofs, logic and sets](../../01-refresher/1.1-proofs-logic-sets/): quantifiers, functions, images and preimages, countability, Zorn's lemma (statement).
- [1.2 Real analysis I](../../01-refresher/1.2-real-analysis-I/): sequences, completeness of $\mathbb{R}$, Bolzano–Weierstrass, continuity, uniform continuity.

## Why this module

Almost every Masters course speaks the language of this module. Measure theory needs open sets and $\sigma$-algebras generated by them; functional analysis is metric-space theory in infinite dimensions (Baire category gives the uniform boundedness and open mapping theorems); algebraic topology and manifolds start from quotient and product topologies.

## Learning objectives

By the end you should be able to:

1. Work fluently with metric spaces: open and closed sets, closure, interior, boundary, convergence, continuity (in $\varepsilon$–$\delta$, sequential and open-set forms).
2. Prove that standard spaces are complete ($\mathbb{R}^n$, $C[a,b]$ with the sup metric, $\ell^p$) and that others are not.
3. State and prove the Baire category theorem and use it (for example, $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable; a complete metric space with no isolated points is uncountable; most continuous functions are nowhere differentiable, as a statement).
4. Prove the equivalence of compactness, sequential compactness, and "complete and totally bounded" for metric spaces.
5. Prove that continuous images of compact (connected) spaces are compact (connected), and use connectedness arguments.
6. Define a topological space, basis, subspace, product and quotient topologies, and compute them in examples.
7. Know the separation axioms ($T_1$, Hausdorff, regular, normal), the statement of Urysohn's lemma and Tychonoff's theorem, and standard counterexamples.

## Checklist of key definitions and theorems

- [ ] Metric, metric space, open ball, open set, closed set, closure, interior, boundary, limit point
- [ ] Equivalent forms of continuity in metric spaces; uniform continuity; Lipschitz maps
- [ ] Equivalent metrics vs topologically equivalent metrics
- [ ] Cauchy sequence, complete metric space; closed subsets of complete spaces are complete
- [ ] Completeness of $\mathbb{R}^n$, $\ell^p$, $C(K)$ with the sup metric
- [ ] Completion of a metric space (statement)
- [ ] Contraction mapping (Banach fixed-point) theorem
- [ ] Nowhere dense, meagre (first category); Baire category theorem
- [ ] Compactness (open covers), sequential compactness, total boundedness; their equivalence in metric spaces
- [ ] Heine–Borel; Lebesgue number lemma; continuous functions on compact sets are bounded, attain extrema and are uniformly continuous
- [ ] Connected, path connected, components; continuous image of connected is connected; intervals are the connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$
- [ ] Topology, basis, subbasis; topology generated by a basis
- [ ] Subspace, product (vs box), quotient topologies; universal properties of product and quotient
- [ ] Homeomorphism; a continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism
- [ ] $T_1$, Hausdorff, regular, normal; metric spaces are normal; compact Hausdorff spaces are normal
- [ ] Urysohn's lemma (statement), Tietze extension theorem (statement), Tychonoff's theorem (statement)

## Exit criteria

You are ready to move on when you can, without notes:

1. Prove the Baire category theorem for complete metric spaces and give two applications.
2. Prove that a metric space is compact iff it is sequentially compact, including the Lebesgue number lemma.
3. Prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, and give a counterexample when either hypothesis fails.
4. Describe the quotient topology on the torus and on $\mathbb{RP}^2$ and explain why the quotient map is open in the first case.
5. Solve at least 4 of the 5 Challenge problems in [problems.md](problems.md) on your own.
