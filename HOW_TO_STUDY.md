# How to study

Advice for working through Masters-level mathematics on your own, in 5–8 hours a week.

## A typical week (about 6 hours)

| Session | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.5 h | Read the week's section of the main text slowly, with paper. Watch the matching lecture if there is one. |
| 2 | 1.5 h | Re-read, filling in every skipped step in the proofs. Read the matching part of `notes.md`. |
| 3 | 2 h | Problems. Work on each one for at least 20–30 minutes before looking at a hint or solution. |
| 4 | 1 h | Write up one or two solutions properly in LaTeX. Review flashcards. Update `PROGRESS.md`. |

Short daily sessions (for example 45 minutes on 6 days) work as well as long ones, and often better for memory.

## Reading mathematics

- Read with pen and paper. Before reading a proof, try to prove the statement yourself for five minutes.
- For every definition, write down an example, a non-example, and why each condition in the definition is needed.
- For every theorem, find out which hypotheses are used where, and look for a counterexample when each one is dropped.
- After each section, close the book and write the main results and the idea of each proof from memory.

## Problems

- Problems are where the learning happens. Reading alone gives a false sense of understanding.
- If you are stuck after a real attempt, look at only the first line of the solution, then try again.
- After solving a problem, compare your solution with the one in `solutions.md` and note any differences in method.
- Keep a "mistakes log" file for each module: errors you made and the idea you missed.

## Writing proofs

- Write up at least one solution a week in full, in LaTeX, using [templates/problem-set.tex](templates/problem-set.tex).
- Write so that a classmate could follow every step without asking you a question.
- Once a month, re-read old write-ups and improve one.

## Remembering

- Use spaced repetition (for example Anki) for definitions and theorem statements. Add 5–10 cards a week, not more.
- At the end of each module, write a one-page summary from memory, then check it against the notes.
- Before starting each new module, spend one session reviewing the prerequisite module's summary.

## Getting feedback

Self-study lacks a marker. Ways to get feedback:

- Math StackExchange (math.stackexchange.com) for specific questions. Show your attempt.
- A proof assistant such as Lean 4 checks proofs exactly. The Natural Number Game and the book *Mathematics in Lean* are gentle starts. This is optional.
- A local university: many allow external students to sit in on lectures or will put you in touch with a tutor.
- A study partner or online reading group working through the same text.

## When it gets hard

- Being stuck is normal at this level. Hours stuck on one problem are not wasted.
- If a whole topic is not making sense, try a second textbook. A different explanation often helps.
- If you fall behind the schedule, extend it. Finishing slowly is better than skipping material you will need later.
