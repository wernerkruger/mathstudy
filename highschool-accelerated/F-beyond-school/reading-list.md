# Reading list: mathematics for reading, ages 13–17

[← Stage F](README.md) · [Track overview](../README.md)

Popular and semi-popular books to read for pleasure alongside the track. They are not required. Leave them around the house; let him choose. All are paid books (library copies are fine); no links are given. The age column is a rough guide for a strong reader; most are in English, and several have Dutch translations.

## Stories and history (from about 13)

| Book | Author | Age | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| *The Number Devil* | Hans Magnus Enzensberger | 12–14 | A boy dreams of a devil who teaches him primes, Fibonacci, infinity. Light, clever, a good first book. |
| *Flatland* | Edwin A. Abbott | 13+ | Victorian novella about life in two dimensions; the best introduction to thinking about higher dimensions. |
| *Fermat's Last Theorem* (US title *Fermat's Enigma*) | Simon Singh | 13+ | The 350-year history of one problem and Andrew Wiles's proof. Gripping. |
| *The Code Book* | Simon Singh | 13+ | History of cryptography from Caesar to RSA, with puzzles. Links to the olympiad number theory. |
| *Uncle Petros and Goldbach's Conjecture* | Apostolos Doxiadis | 14+ | A novel about a mathematician obsessed with one problem. Also about what a life in mathematics is like. |
| *The Man Who Knew Infinity* | Robert Kanigel | 15+ | Biography of Ramanujan and his collaboration with Hardy. |
| *Journey through Genius* | William Dunham | 14+ | Twelve great theorems with their full proofs and history, from Euclid to Cantor. Very good for this track. |
| *An Imaginary Tale: The Story of √−1* | Paul J. Nahin | 15+ | The history of complex numbers, with real mathematics. Read during or after E2. |
| *Euclid's Window* | Leonard Mlodinow | 14+ | The history of geometry, from Euclid to Einstein and string theory. |
| *The Music of the Primes* | Marcus du Sautoy | 15+ | The Riemann hypothesis and the people who worked on it. |

## Ideas and how mathematicians think (from about 14)

| Book | Author | Age | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| *The Joy of x* | Steven Strogatz | 13+ | Short chapters on everything from numbers to calculus to topology. |
| *Infinite Powers* | Steven Strogatz | 14+ | The story and ideas of calculus. A perfect companion to stages C–E. |
| *Alex's Adventures in Numberland* | Alex Bellos | 13+ | Numbers, cultures, puzzles; fun. |
| *Humble Pi* | Matt Parker | 13+ | Real-world disasters caused by mathematical mistakes. Funny, and teaches care. |
| *Things to Make and Do in the Fourth Dimension* | Matt Parker | 13+ | Hands-on recreational mathematics. |
| *How Not to Be Wrong* | Jordan Ellenberg | 15+ | Mathematical thinking applied to everyday questions, statistics and politics. |
| *How to Bake Pi* | Eugenia Cheng | 14+ | What abstract mathematics (category theory) is about, explained through recipes. |
| *A Mathematician's Lament* | Paul Lockhart | 14+ | A short, passionate essay on what mathematics really is and how school gets it wrong. |
| *Measurement* | Paul Lockhart | 15+ | Geometry and calculus discovered through beautiful problems; very much in the spirit of this track. |
| *17 Equations That Changed the World* | Ian Stewart | 14+ | From Pythagoras to Black–Scholes; one equation per chapter. |
| *Professor Stewart's Cabinet of Mathematical Curiosities* | Ian Stewart | 13+ | Puzzles, paradoxes and short stories; good for dipping in. |
| Martin Gardner's collections of *Mathematical Games* columns | Martin Gardner | 13+ | The classic recreational mathematics; any volume. |

## Deeper books (from about 16)

| Book | Author | Age | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Mathematics: A Very Short Introduction* | Timothy Gowers | 15+ | A Fields medallist explains abstraction, proof and infinity in 150 pages. |
| *A Mathematician's Apology* | G. H. Hardy | 16+ | Hardy's famous essay on the beauty of pure mathematics. Read with C. P. Snow's foreword. |
| *How to Solve It* | George Pólya | 14+ | The classic on problem-solving heuristics. Also used in the olympiad track. |
| *What Is Mathematics?* | Richard Courant and Herbert Robbins | 16+ | A real mathematics book, broad and deep. Dip in for years. |
| *Proofs from THE BOOK* | Martin Aigner and Günter M. Ziegler | 16+ | The most elegant proofs in mathematics. Some chapters are within reach after stage E. |
| *Gödel, Escher, Bach* | Douglas Hofstadter | 16+ | Logic, self-reference, Escher and Bach. Long and playful; not for everyone, unforgettable for some. |
| *Visual Complex Analysis* | Tristan Needham | 16+ | Textbook, but readable for pleasure after E2: complex numbers through pictures. |
| *Gödel's Proof* | Ernest Nagel and James R. Newman | 16+ | A short, careful explanation of the incompleteness theorems. |
