# Stage F: Beyond school (klas 5–6, 2031–33)

[← Track overview](../README.md) · [Reading list](reading-list.md) · [Parent guide](../PARENT_GUIDE.md) · [Progress](../PROGRESS.md)

**When:** klas 5 (2031–32) and klas 6 (2032–33), age about 17–18.
**Time:** about 4 hours a week, as before, plus the olympiad if he continues.
**Previous stage:** [E Wiskunde D and exams](../E-wiskunde-d-and-exams/).

By now the school programme is covered. Stage F has four goals:

1. finish the school side well, possibly with an **early central exam** in klas 5;
2. take part in a **pre-university programme** at a university;
3. continue the **olympiad** as far as he wants to go;
4. learn **real university mathematics**: proofs, linear algebra, analysis, algebra, using the parent's adult track.

Stage F is deliberately less prescriptive than A–E. At 17 he should increasingly choose for himself.

## 1. The early central exam

The plan and the legal basis are in the [stage E overview](../E-wiskunde-d-and-exams/README.md#planning-a-possible-early-central-exam-in-klas-5-may-2032). In short:

- The law (WVO 2020, art. 2.56, lid 4) allows the central exam in a subject one or two years early (VWO klas 4 or 5), if the school and the subject teacher agree.
- The school exam (*schoolexamen*) for the subject must be finished first, and an early result lapses if he is not promoted.
- The first central exam of the new programme (wiskunde techniek) is probably in **May 2032**, so **klas 5** is the realistic early date.

**Process in klas 5 (if agreed in klas 4):**

| When | Step |
|---|---|
| September 2031 | Confirm the agreement in writing with the *examensecretaris* / *decaan*: which subject, which *tijdvak*, and the adjusted *PTA* (school-exam schedule). |
| September 2031 – March 2032 | Complete all school-exam tests for the subject. Keep doing one timed past paper or sample exam every two to three weeks (see [E4](../E-wiskunde-d-and-exams/E4-exam-practice/)), plus any wiskunde techniek sample exams the CvTE publishes. |
| Before registration deadline (school sets it, usually winter) | The school registers him for the CE in that subject. |
| May 2032 | Central exam (*tijdvak 1*). The resit (*tijdvak 2*) in June is available as for any candidate; check the school's rules for early candidates. |
| Klas 6 | The freed mathematics hours can be used for university courses or the *profielwerkstuk*, as agreed with the school. |

If the school does not agree, nothing is lost: he sits the CE in klas 6 with the regular cohort, well prepared, and spends the extra time on the material below.

Wiskunde D (or its successor) has no central exam; it is finished through the school exam alone. If the school offers it, ask whether its school exam can be completed in klas 5.

## 2. Pre-university programmes

These let bovenbouw VWO pupils follow real university courses, sometimes for credit. Check each year's conditions and deadlines; many work through partner schools, so ask the school's *decaan* first.

| Programme | Who | What | Link |
|---|---|---|---|

| Leiden University **Pre-Classes** and other "extra challenge" activities | Bovenbouw VWO | Short courses and activities | <https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/scholen/scholieren-en-wetenschap/extra-uitdaging-voor-leerlingen> |
| **Radboud Pre-University College** (Nijmegen) | Bovenbouw VWO | Follow up to two first-year university courses per semester | <https://www.ru.nl/opleidingen/meer-onderwijs-en-trainingen/programmas-voor-leerlingen/extra-uitdaging> |
| **Radboud Pre-University College of Science** | Bovenbouw VWO with a science interest | Science programme with research projects | <https://www.ru.nl/en/departments/institute-for-science-education/radboud-pre-university-college-of-science> |

Other universities (for example the technical universities and Utrecht, Amsterdam, Groningen) often have their own programmes for talented pupils; search their websites for "scholieren" or "pre-university", or ask at open days. A first-year course such as *Lineaire Algebra 1* or *Analyse 1* taken as a pupil is the best preparation there is, and is sometimes recognised later.

## 3. Olympiad

The competition track continues in the [olympiad folder](../olympiad/). In klas 5–6:

- Nederlandse Wiskunde Olympiade: first round at school (January), second round at universities (March), final in September at TU/e. Finalists are invited to free training at universities and can be selected for the international teams (IMO and others). He must still be in school to compete, so klas 5 and klas 6 are his last chances.
- Advanced olympiad material is in modules O1–O5 of the olympiad track. From klas 5 the training camps take over much of this.
- If he does not enjoy olympiad mathematics, that is fine: university mathematics (section 4) is a different skill, slower and deeper. Many excellent mathematicians never did olympiads.

## 4. Bridge to university mathematics

University mathematics is about **definitions and proofs**, not calculation. The parent's own adult track in this repository covers the first two years of a mathematics degree. Recommended two-year sequence for him, at about 4 hours a week (about 38 weeks a year):

| Order | Module (adult track) | Suggested timing | Main book |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [1.1 Proofs, logic and sets](../../adult-masters/01-refresher/1.1-proofs-logic-sets/) | Klas 5, September–November | Velleman, *How to Prove It*; or Hammack, *Book of Proof* (free) |
| 2 | [1.3 Linear algebra](../../adult-masters/01-refresher/1.3-linear-algebra/) | Klas 5, December–April | Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right* (free) |
| 3 | [1.2 Real analysis I](../../adult-masters/01-refresher/1.2-real-analysis-I/) | Klas 5 May – klas 6 November | Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*; Spivak, *Calculus* |
| 4 | [1.4 Abstract algebra I](../../adult-masters/01-refresher/1.4-abstract-algebra-I/) | Klas 6, December–March | Pinter, *A Book of Abstract Algebra* |
| 5 | [2.2 Complex analysis](../../adult-masters/02-bridge/2.2-complex-analysis/) | Klas 6, April–July (after the exams) | Needham, *Visual Complex Analysis* |

Optional, if time allows or instead of one of the above: [1.5 Multivariable calculus](../../adult-masters/01-refresher/1.5-multivariable-calculus/), [2.1 Metric spaces and topology](../../adult-masters/02-bridge/2.1-metric-spaces-topology/), [2.3 Abstract algebra II](../../adult-masters/02-bridge/2.3-abstract-algebra-II/).

**Why this order.** Proofs first, because everything after depends on writing them. Linear algebra next: concrete enough to be friendly, abstract enough to train thinking in definitions, and needed everywhere. Real analysis then makes rigorous the calculus he already knows (limits, continuity, the mean value theorem he used in E1). Abstract algebra builds on proofs and linear algebra. Complex analysis comes last and connects back to E2.

**How to study.** The adult-track modules are written for an adult; the mathematics is the same. Advice:

- Read with pencil and paper. Reproduce each proof without the book a day later.
- Do exercises every week; aim for quality over quantity.
- Find a mentor who can read his proofs: a teacher, a university student, or a lecturer via a pre-university programme. Unchecked proofs are the main weakness of self-study.
- Do not rush. Six months of solid linear algebra is worth more than a fast tour through five subjects.

**Recommended books** (paid unless marked free):

| Book | Use |
|---|---|
| Daniel J. Velleman, *How to Prove It: A Structured Approach* | Proof writing, logic, sets, induction |
| Richard Hammack, *Book of Proof* (free PDF): <https://www.people.vcu.edu/~rhammack/BookOfProof/> | Alternative to Velleman, very clear |
| Sheldon Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right*, 4th ed. (free, open access): <https://linear.axler.net/> | Linear algebra the way mathematicians see it |
| Michael Spivak, *Calculus* | Rigorous calculus with famous hard exercises; a long-term companion |
| Stephen Abbott, *Understanding Analysis* | The friendliest first real-analysis book |
| Charles C. Pinter, *A Book of Abstract Algebra* | Groups, rings, fields, gently |
| Tristan Needham, *Visual Complex Analysis* | Geometric complex analysis; continues E2 |
| George Pólya, *How to Solve It* | Problem-solving heuristics; short, classic |
| Richard Courant and Herbert Robbins, *What Is Mathematics?* | A broad, deep tour; dip in anywhere |

For lighter reading see the [reading list](reading-list.md).

## 5. Profielwerkstuk ideas in mathematics

Every VWO pupil writes a *profielwerkstuk* (PWS, a research project of about 80 hours) in klas 5–6, usually in pairs. A mathematics PWS should contain real mathematics (definitions, a proof or derivation, computation) and an own contribution (an experiment, a program, a generalisation). Ideas, many building on stage E:

1. **Which regular polygons can be constructed with ruler and compass?** Gauss and the 17-gon, via roots of unity (E2).
2. **Chaos in the logistic map.** Period doubling, Feigenbaum's constant, measured with your own program (E1).
3. **The Mandelbrot set and Julia sets.** Complex dynamics; prove that the Mandelbrot set lies in the disc $\lvert c\rvert \le 2$ (E2).
4. **Epidemic models.** The SIR system of differential equations, Euler vs Runge–Kutta, fitted to real data (E1).
5. **Kepler's laws from Newton's law of gravitation.** Why orbits are conic sections (E3).
6. **Public-key cryptography.** Modular arithmetic, Fermat's little theorem, RSA; implement it ([olympiad O2](../olympiad/O2-number-theory/)).
7. **Error-correcting codes.** Hamming codes, QR codes; linear algebra over $\{0, 1\}$.
8. **Seat allocation in the Tweede Kamer.** The D'Hondt method compared with other methods; Arrow's impossibility theorem.
9. **Benford's law.** Why first digits are not uniform; test on Dutch municipality data (CBS).
10. **Shortest routes in the Dutch railway network.** Graph theory and Dijkstra's algorithm.
11. **Escher and tilings.** Symmetry groups of the plane (the 17 wallpaper groups), hyperbolic tilings.
12. **Fourier series and sound.** Decompose real recordings, explain MP3-style compression.
13. **Monte Carlo methods.** Estimating $\pi$ and integrals by random sampling, with error analysis via the $\sqrt n$-law ([D4](../D-bovenbouw-advanced/D4-probability-statistics/)).
14. **Random walks and the gambler's ruin.** Recurrences (E1) and probability.
15. **The isoperimetric problem.** Why the circle encloses the largest area for a given perimeter; proofs for polygons.
16. **Game theory.** Nash equilibria, a real-world game analysed.
17. **Numerical stability.** When does Euler's method give nonsense? Stiff equations.

Wiskunde D domain F (*wiskunde in wetenschap*) also asks for applying mathematics in science; a PWS topic can sometimes count for both. Discuss with the supervisor early.

## 6. Choosing a university mathematics programme

**In the Netherlands.** Bachelor's programmes in mathematics (*Wiskunde*) are offered at, among others, Leiden, Utrecht, Amsterdam (UvA and VU), Groningen and Radboud Nijmegen; the technical universities Delft and Twente offer *Technische Wiskunde* (applied mathematics), and Eindhoven offers *Applied Mathematics*. Many universities offer **double bachelor's** degrees (*dubbele bachelor*) with physics, computer science or another subject, and **honours programmes** for strong students. Compare them on <https://www.studiekeuze123.nl/>.

Things to compare:

- **Pure vs applied emphasis**, and how much freedom there is in the second and third year.
- **Teaching style**: size of tutorial groups, amount of proof-writing in year 1.
- **Double bachelor and honours options.**
- **Language**: some programmes are taught (partly) in English.
- **The *bindend studieadvies*** (binding study advice): the minimum credits needed in year 1 to continue.
- **Feel**: go to open days (*open dagen*) and trial-study days (*proefstuderen*, *meeloopdagen*) in klas 5.

**Admission.** Dutch mathematics bachelor's programmes require a VWO diploma with the right mathematics subject; today that means **Wiskunde B**, under the new programmes probably **wiskunde techniek**. Some programmes also require physics (*natuurkunde*). Check the exact requirements per programme on the university website in klas 4 or 5, before final subject choices are fixed.

**Abroad.** Some pupils consider universities in the UK, Germany or Switzerland. UK universities such as Cambridge and Oxford use extra mathematics admission tests (Cambridge: STEP; Oxford: MAT) with deadlines in the autumn of the final school year; a Dutch pupil would apply in klas 6. Check current requirements on the universities' own websites well in advance; this repository has not verified them.

**Starting early.** Pupils who followed university courses via a pre-university programme can sometimes get exemptions (*vrijstellingen*). Ask the programme's exam board (*examencommissie*).
