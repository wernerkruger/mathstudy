**When:** klas 2 of gymnasium, school year 2028–29 (he is about 14).
**Length:** 34 study weeks of about 4 hours, plus 4 buffer weeks (38 weeks in total).
**Previous stage:** [B: onderbouw compact](../B-onderbouw-compact/) · **Next stage:** [D: bovenbouw advanced](../D-bovenbouw-advanced/)

## What changes in this stage

Until now he has been compacting lower-school mathematics (*onderbouw*). From this stage on he works on **upper-school content** (*bovenbouw*): the material that VWO pupils normally meet in klas 4 and 5 and that is tested in the central exam (*centraal examen*, CE).

His formal programme in the upper school will be **wiskunde techniek**, the successor of Wiskunde B for the Natuur en Techniek profile (first klas 4 cohort: September 2029; he reaches klas 4 in September 2030). Wiskunde techniek keeps the whole Wiskunde B core that this stage covers: standard functions, equations and inequalities, asymptotes, differentiation, and trigonometric functions. So nothing here will be wasted. Where these modules refer to exam codes, they use the codes of the current Wiskunde B syllabus (CvTE syllabus 2028) because those codes are stable and past exam papers are organised around them; each module README also names the matching wiskunde techniek domain.

The style also changes:

- **Exact work** (*exact berekenen*). Upper-school exams distinguish "bereken" (a graphing calculator, *grafische rekenmachine* or GR, is allowed) from "bereken exact" (algebra only, answers like $\tfrac{1}{3}\sqrt{3}$ or $\ln 2$). From now on he should do every problem exactly first and use a calculator or GeoGebra only to check.
- **"Toon aan"** (show that). Many CE questions give the answer and ask for a convincing derivation. Treat these as small proofs: every step must follow from the previous one, and the conclusion must be written down.
- **Proofs of the rules.** The notes prove the logarithm rules, the derivative rules and the addition formulas. He should be able to reproduce these proofs, not only use the rules.

| [C4 Trigonometric functions](C4-trigonometric-functions/) | 8 | radians, unit circle, graphs of sin/cos/tan, period, amplitude and equilibrium, exact values, equations with general solutions, identities, sine and cosine rules | D, E1 | A4 verbanden, A6 meetkunde |
| Buffer | 4 | catch-up, review, mixed past-exam questions | | |

Total: 34 weeks + 4 buffer = 38 weeks.

The order matters. C1 builds the language of functions that everything else uses. C2 needs inverse functions from C1. C3 needs power functions and rational functions from C1; it deliberately leaves out the chain rule (*kettingregel*) and the derivatives of $e^x$, $\ln x$ and $\sin x$, which come in [stage D](../D-bovenbouw-advanced/) (module [D1 Differential calculus II](../D-bovenbouw-advanced/D1-differential-calculus-II/)). C4 can be studied without C3, so if he gets stuck in C3 he can switch to C4 for a few weeks.

## How to use the module folders

Each module has the same four files:

1. `README.md`: objectives, exam mapping, resources, week plan, "I can…" checklist and exit criteria.
2. `notes.md`: read actively, with pen and paper. Reproduce every worked example before reading the solution.
3. `problems.md`: Practice problems first, then CE-level, then Challenge. Not every Challenge problem has to be solved in the planned week.
4. `solutions.md`: only after a real attempt (at least 15 minutes for a hard problem). Compare not only the answer but also how the solution is written down.

## Stage exit criteria

He is ready for stage D when:

- all four module checklists are ticked and each module's exit criteria are met;
- he can do a full past CE Wiskunde B paper's questions on functions, exponentials and logarithms, basic differentiation and trigonometry (leave out integrals, chain rule and vectors) with at least 70% of the points according to the *correctievoorschrift*;
- he can reproduce, without notes, the proofs of: the product rule for logarithms, the derivative of $x^n$ for positive integers $n$, the product rule for derivatives, and the formula for $\cos(\alpha - \beta)$.
