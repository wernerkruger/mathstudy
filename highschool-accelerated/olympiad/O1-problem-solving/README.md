# O1 Problem-solving strategies (probleemaanpak)

This is the first module of the competition-maths track. It is not about new mathematical content. It is about *how to attack a problem you have never seen before*: what to try first, what to try when you are stuck, and how to write down an answer that convinces someone else.

## When

- **School year:** groep 8 (October 2026) through the first months of klas 1 (until about January 2028).
- **Time:** about 30 sessions of roughly 1 hour, one per week. The plan below has a natural pause between April and August 2027, after the Kangoeroe competition; use that time for other modules of the track or light practice.
- **Competitions it prepares for:**
  - **W4Kangoeroe, category wizSMART** (groep 7–8), March 2027. Multiple choice, speed and cleverness matter.
  - **Nederlandse Wiskunde Olympiade (NWO), first round** (eerste ronde), January 2028, in klas 1. Short-answer problems; later rounds also ask for written proofs.
  - Level 3 problems point further ahead: the NWO final round and the Benelux Mathematical Olympiad (BxMO). They are stretch problems, not requirements.

## Objectives

By the end of the module you should be able to:

- Use Polya's four steps (vier stappen van Polya): understand the problem, make a plan, carry out the plan, look back (terugkijken).
- Work backwards (terugredeneren) from the end state, including finding winning positions in simple games (winnende en verliezende posities).
- Try small cases (kleine gevallen), spot a pattern (patroon), and then explain *why* the pattern holds.
- Organise information with a diagram (tekening), a table (tabel) or a Venn diagram (Venndiagram), and count systematically (systematisch tellen).
- Use parity (pariteit, even/oneven) to show that something is impossible.
- Find an invariant (invariant) or a colouring (kleuring) that does not change during a process.
- Apply the pigeonhole principle (ladenprincipe / duiventilprincipe), including its stronger form.
- Use the extremal principle (extremaal principe): look at the largest, smallest, longest or closest object.
- Solve logic puzzles (logische puzzels): knights and knaves (ridders en schurken), truth-tellers and liars, and grid logic (logigrammen).
- Write a short, complete argument (bewijs) that another person can check line by line.

## How to use the files

- [notes.md](notes.md): read the section for the week *before* the session. Cover each worked example and try it yourself first; then compare with the solution.
- [problems.md](problems.md): the weekly problems. Give each problem a real attempt (at least 15 minutes for Level 2 and 3) before looking at the hint list at the end of the file.
- [solutions.md](solutions.md): only open after a serious attempt. If your answer is right but your method is different, write both down; comparing methods is where much of the learning happens.
- Keep a **problem notebook** (probleemschrift). For each problem write: what you tried, where you got stuck, and the key idea that finally worked. Before a competition, reread only the key ideas.
- In the "Look back" step, ask: could I have solved it faster? Does the same idea solve another problem in the list?
- For Kangoeroe-style questions, practise eliminating wrong options and estimating; for NWO-style questions, practise writing a full argument.

## Resources

Books:

- George Polya, *How to Solve It*: the origin of the four steps. Short and readable.
- Paul Zeitz, *The Art and Craft of Problem Solving*: chapters on strategies, invariants, pigeonhole and the extremal principle. Pitched a bit higher; good for Level 3.
- Arthur Engel, *Problem-Solving Strategies*: a large collection of olympiad problems by technique. Use chapters 1 (invariants) and 4 (box principle) as a source of extra problems later.
- Art of Problem Solving (AoPS), *Prealgebra* and *Introduction to Counting & Probability*: clear explanations with many exercises.
- Raymond Smullyan, logic-puzzle books (for example *What Is the Name of This Book?*): knights, knaves and many variations.

Online:

- Past Kangoeroe papers with answers, in Dutch and English: https://www.w4kangoeroe.nl/kangoeroe/index.php/historie
- NWO past papers and solutions (wedstrijdarchief): https://www.wiskundeolympiade.nl/wedstrijdarchief
- Pythagoras Olympiade problems and solutions: https://www.pyth.eu/olympiade
- AoPS Alcumus (free adaptive practice): https://artofproblemsolving.com/alcumus
- AoPS curriculum overview: https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/list/aops-curriculum
- Evan Chen, *An Infinitely Large Napkin* (for much later, after VWO): https://web.evanchen.cc/napkin.html
