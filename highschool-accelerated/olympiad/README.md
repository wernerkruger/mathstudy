# Competition maths track (wiskundewedstrijden)

This track runs alongside the main programme from groep 8 to VWO 6, at about **1 hour a week**. It is separate from school maths. School maths (and the main track) teaches you to use tools that someone else has chosen for you. Competition maths teaches you to attack a problem you have never seen before: choose an approach, get stuck, try another, and finally write a solution that convinces a jury.

## Purpose

- **Thinking, not speed.** Olympiad problems test creativity and insight more than knowledge. The NWO (Nederlandse Wiskunde Olympiade) itself says the first round suits onderbouw pupils for that reason.
- **Writing proofs.** From the NWO second round onwards, an answer without a full argument earns few points. Writing complete solutions is the most useful skill this track builds, and it pays off later in university mathematics.
- **Breadth.** The four classic olympiad areas (number theory, combinatorics, geometry, algebra) go well beyond the VWO syllabus and fill in what school leaves out.
- **Fun and company.** Rounds at a university, training days and finals are where he meets other children who like the same things. That matters at least as much as the results.

Results are a by-product. The aim is that he enjoys hard problems and keeps doing them for years.

### His first chances

- **Kangoeroe:** March 2027, wizSMART (groep 8). He may sit wizBRAIN instead if the school allows; I'd suggest wizSMART for a first, confidence-building go, and the wizBRAIN paper at home afterwards.
- **NWO first round:** he can't enter in January 2027 because he is still in groep 8. **His first chance is January 2028, in klas 1.** His last first round is January 2032 (klas 5); the final for that cohort is in September 2032, in klas 6.
- **JWO:** first possible in June 2028 (klas 1), through a top-200 Kangoeroe score in March 2028; again in June 2029 (klas 2).
- **Pythagoras Olympiade:** he can start any time. The two easier problems per issue are a good monthly exercise from groep 8 onwards.

## How to practise

1. **Keep a problem journal (probleemschrift).** One notebook or folder. For each problem write the date, the source, how long it took, and what the key idea turned out to be. Once a month read back through it.
2. **Write full solutions.** Write every solution as if a jury will mark it: what is claimed, why each step holds, and why the answer is complete ("and there are no other solutions"). From O2 on, a numerical answer without an argument doesn't count as solved.
3. **Time-box.** Give a problem a fixed slot (about 20 minutes at Level 1–2, 45–60 minutes at Level 3). If stuck, read only the hint, then give it another slot. Read the full solution only after a real attempt, and then rewrite the solution yourself the next day without looking.
4. **Review.** After every session, write one sentence: "Next time I see ..., I'll try ...". Redo problems you failed two weeks later.
5. **Mock papers.** Before each competition, sit one or two past papers under real conditions (same time limit, no calculator if not allowed), then mark them with the official solutions.
6. **Talk about it.** Explaining a solution to a parent, teacher or friend shows quickly whether it is really complete. A school maths club or plusklas helps.
7. **Keep it at an hour.** One focused hour a week beats three hours once a month.
