# O4 Olympiad geometry (olympiademeetkunde)

Synthetic (Euclidean) geometry the way it appears in the NWO and the BxMO: angle chasing, circles, triangle centres, lengths and areas. The aim is to see the right auxiliary circle or similar pair of triangles quickly, and then to write a proof that a jury accepts.

## When

- **School year:** klas 3, 2029–30 (age 15).
- **Length:** about 34 weeks at roughly 1 hour per week.
- **Competition targets:** NWO (Nederlandse Wiskunde Olympiade) first round in January 2030, second round in March 2030, the NWO final in September 2030, and, if he is selected, the team training and the BxMO (Benelux Mathematical Olympiad). In the NWO, geometry is roughly one problem in four, and in the final and the BxMO it is usually one full proof problem. The week numbers in the plan below are approximate; move the mock weeks so they land one or two weeks before the actual competition dates.

## Prerequisites

- [O1 Problem-solving strategies](../O1-problem-solving/): working backwards, looking at extreme cases, and writing a clean argument.
- [O2 Number theory](../O2-number-theory/) and [O3 Combinatorics](../O3-combinatorics/): not needed for the geometry itself, but the mock papers mix all four areas, so he should keep them warm.
- Main track: B3 Geometry and trigonometry in [B onderbouw compact](../../B-onderbouw-compact/). He must be fluent with Pythagoras, similar triangles, the basic trigonometric ratios (sinus, cosinus, tangens) and the idea of a proof (bewijs) from given facts.

## Objectives

By the end of this module he can:

- Chase angles (hoeken jagen) with parallel lines (evenwijdige lijnen), the angle sum of a triangle (hoekensom), exterior angles (buitenhoeken) and isosceles triangles (gelijkbenige driehoeken).
- Prove triangles congruent (congruent) or similar (gelijkvormig), and use the angle bisector theorem (bissectricestelling).
- Use the inscribed angle theorem (stelling van de omtrekshoek), Thales' theorem (stelling van Thales) and the tangent–chord angle (hoek tussen raaklijn en koorde).
- Prove that four points are concyclic (liggen op één cirkel), that is, that a quadrilateral is cyclic (koordenvierhoek), in several ways, and know when directed angles (georiënteerde hoeken) are needed.
- Use the power of a point (macht van een punt), the radical axis (machtlijn) and the radical centre (machtpunt).
- Work with the triangle centres: centroid (zwaartepunt), circumcentre (middelpunt van de omgeschreven cirkel), incentre (middelpunt van de ingeschreven cirkel), excentres (middelpunten van de aangeschreven cirkels) and orthocentre (hoogtepunt). State the Euler line (rechte van Euler) and prove and use the incentre/excentre lemma.
- Compute areas (oppervlakten) with ratio arguments, Heron's formula (formule van Heron), $\frac12 ab\sin C$, $rs$ and $\frac{abc}{4R}$.
- Use the sine rule (sinusregel) with $2R$, the cosine rule (cosinusregel), Stewart's theorem (stelling van Stewart) and the median length formula (lengte van een zwaartelijn).
- State and prove Ceva's theorem (stelling van Ceva) and Menelaus' theorem (stelling van Menelaos), and use their converses to prove concurrency (concurrentie) and collinearity (collineariteit).
- Write a complete geometry proof that names every configuration assumption, as required in the NWO final and the BxMO.

## How to use the files

- **Always draw a large, accurate diagram.** Use a compass and ruler (passer en liniaal) on a full A4 sheet, or GeoGebra (free, in the browser or as an app). A small sketch hides the truth; a large accurate one shows you which points are concyclic, which lines are equal and which angles match. Then draw a second diagram with a different shape of triangle, to see which facts are general and which were accidents of the first picture.
- **Measure, then prove.** In GeoGebra, measure angles and lengths to make a conjecture, and drag the points to test it. The measurement is a guide, never a proof.
- **[notes.md](notes.md)**: read one section before each week's session. Each section starts with a "Draw it" instruction; do it before reading further. Try every worked example yourself before reading the solution.
- **[problems.md](problems.md)**: the problems for the week are listed in the plan above. Spend at least 15 minutes on a problem before opening a hint, and write a final answer or a full proof before checking.
- **Hints**: one line per problem at the end of problems.md. Use one hint, then try again for 10 minutes.
- **[solutions.md](solutions.md)**: read the solution only after a serious attempt. For proof problems, check that your proof says where every point lies (inside or outside a segment, same or opposite side of a line), or uses directed angles so that it does not matter.
- **Error log**: keep one page listing every mistake (wrong configuration, an angle read from the picture without proof, a ratio turned upside down). Read it before each mock.
- **Mocks**: use real past papers from the NWO archive, under exam conditions with a timer and no notes. Mark them using the official solutions.

## Resources

Books (no links needed):

- Richard Rusczyk, *Introduction to Geometry* (AoPS). The main companion for weeks 1–15; the chapters on circles, power of a point and areas match sections 1–6 of the notes.
- H. S. M. Coxeter and S. L. Greitzer, *Geometry Revisited*. Short, classic chapters on Ceva, Menelaus, the Euler line and cyclic quadrilaterals.
- Evan Chen, *Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads* (the "EGMO book"). Chapters 1–3 (angle chasing, circles, lengths and ratios) are the target level for the NWO final and the BxMO. It also explains directed angles carefully.
- Yufei Zhao's olympiad geometry handouts (for example on cyclic quadrilaterals and on power of a point and the radical axis). Search by name; they are free.
