### 3.

(b) Add $2x$: $7 = 5x - 8$. Add 8: $15 = 5x$, so $x = 3$. Check: $7 - 6 = 1$ and $9 - 8 = 1$ ✓.

(c) $4x = 14$, $x = 3\tfrac12$. Check: $21 - 5 = 16$ and $7 + 9 = 16$ ✓.

## School-exam level

### 9.

(a) $K_A = 12 + 0.10m$ and $K_B = 20 + 0.05m$.

(b) $12 + 0.10m = 20 + 0.05m \Rightarrow 0.05m = 8 \Rightarrow m = 160$. At **160 minutes** both cost €28.

(c) Both graphs are straight lines; $A$ starts lower ($12$) but is steeper ($0.10$ per minute), so the lines cross at $m = 160$. At $m = 250$: $K_A = 37$ and $K_B = 32.50$. **Plan B is cheaper.**

### 10.

Let $a$ be Anna's age now. Her brother is $a - 4$. In 5 years: $(a + 5) + (a - 4 + 5) = 40$, so $2a + 6 = 40$, $a = 17$.

Check: now 17 and 13; in 5 years 22 and 18, sum 40 ✓. **Anna is 17 and her brother is 13.**

### 11.

Let the width be $w$ cm; the length is $w + 5$. Perimeter: $2(w + w + 5) = 46$, so $4w + 10 = 46$, $w = 9$.

**Width 9 cm, length 14 cm.** Check: $2\cdot(9 + 14) = 46$ ✓.

### 12.

Let the smallest be $n$: $n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) + (n + 3) = 154$, so $4n + 6 = 154$, $n = 37$. **37, 38, 39, 40.** Check: $37 + 38 + 39 + 40 = 154$ ✓.

### 13.

Let $t$ be the time in hours. Together they cover 60 km: $18t + 22t = 60$, $40t = 60$, $t = 1.5$.

They meet after **1½ hours**, when the cyclist from A has ridden $18 \cdot 1.5 =$ **27 km** from A. (Check: the other has ridden $22\cdot 1.5 = 33$ km, and $27 + 33 = 60$ ✓.)

### 14.

(a) $N = 7\cdot 25 - 30 = 145$ chirps per minute.

(b) $N + 30 = 7T$, so $T = \dfrac{N + 30}{7}$.

(c) $T = \dfrac{110 + 30}{7} = \dfrac{140}{7} = 20$ °C.

(d) $N = 0$ when $T = \tfrac{30}{7} \approx 4.3$ °C. Below about **4.3 °C** the formula gives a negative number of chirps, which means the crickets are silent. (In reality such a rule of thumb only works in a limited range of temperatures.)

### 15.

(a) Pairs from both ends add to $201$; there are 200 terms. $\dfrac{200\cdot 201}{2} = 20\,100$.

(b) $2 + 4 + \dots + 200 = 2(1 + 2 + \dots + 100) = 2\cdot 5050 = 10\,100$.

(c) The terms are $4n - 1$ for $n = 1, \dots, 50$ (since $4\cdot 50 - 1 = 199$): 50 terms. Writing it twice, forwards and backwards, each pair adds to $3 + 199 = 202$. So the sum is $\dfrac{50\cdot 202}{2} = 5050$.

### 16.

Let $x$ be the number of 20-cent coins; then there are $25 - x$ fifty-cent coins. In cents: $20x + 50(25 - x) = 860$, so $1250 - 30x = 860$, $30x = 390$, $x = 13$.

**13 coins of 20 cents and 12 of 50 cents.** Check: $260 + 600 = 860$ ✓.

### 17.

Let $n$ be the number of pupils. Money in: $25n$. Costs: $450 + 12n$. We need $25n \ge 450 + 12n$, so $13n \ge 450$, $n \ge 34.6\ldots$ The number of pupils is whole, so **at least 35 pupils**.

Check: 35 pupils pay €875 and the costs are $450 + 420 = 870$ ✓; 34 pupils pay €850 and the costs are €858 ✗.

### 18.

(a) Slope $a = \dfrac{20 - 11}{5 - 2} = 3$. Then $11 = 3\cdot 2 + b$ gives $b = 5$: $y = 3x + 5$.

(b) $3\cdot 10 + 5 = 35 \ne 36$: **no**.

(c) $3x + 5 = 0$ gives $x = -\tfrac53$.

### 19.

(a) $3x - 6 + 6 = 3x$, so $3x = 3x$, and then $0 = 0$. True for **every** $x$: both sides are the same expression.

(b) $4x - 2x - 2 = 2x + 5$, so $2x - 2 = 2x + 5$, and then $-2 = 5$. Never true: **no solution**.

(c) The right side is $5x + 10$. The equation $5x + c = 5x + 10$ becomes $c = 10$. If $c = 10$, every $x$ is a solution; if $c \ne 10$, there is no solution.

## Challenge

### 20.

(a) $1, 4, 9, 16$: the squares. Guess: $1 + 3 + \dots + (2n - 1) = n^2$.

(b) Write the sum $S$ twice, once backwards:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
S &=& 1 &+& 3 &+& \dots &+& (2n - 1)\\
S &=& (2n - 1) &+& (2n - 3) &+& \dots &+& 1
\end{array}
$$

There are $n$ terms, and each column adds to $2n$. So $2S = n\cdot 2n$, $S = n^2$. $\square$

(c) Build an $n$ by $n$ square of dots in L-shaped layers starting from a corner: the corner dot (1), then an L of 3 dots making a 2 by 2 square, then an L of 5 making 3 by 3, and so on. Going from a $(k-1)$ by $(k-1)$ square to a $k$ by $k$ square adds $k + (k - 1) = 2k - 1$ dots. So $1 + 3 + 5 + \dots + (2n - 1)$ dots fill the $n$ by $n$ square: $n^2$. $\square$

### 21.

$2n + 7 = 2(n + 1) + 5$, so

$$
\frac{2n + 7}{n + 1} = 2 + \frac{5}{n + 1}.
$$

This is an integer exactly when $n + 1$ divides 5, that is $n + 1 \in \lbrace 1, -1, 5, -5\rbrace$. So $n \in \lbrace 0, -2, 4, -6\rbrace$.

Check: $n = 0$: $7$; $n = -2$: $\tfrac{3}{-1} = -3$; $n = 4$: $\tfrac{15}{5} = 3$; $n = -6$: $\tfrac{-5}{-5} = 1$. All integers ✓.

### 22.

(a) Call the number $x$. The steps give $4x$, $4x + 12$, $\tfrac{4x + 12}{2} = 2x + 6$, and $2x + 6 - 2x = 6$. Always 6, whatever $x$ was. $\square$

(b) $(x + 3)(x - 3) + 9 = x^2 - 9 + 9 = x^2$. $\square$

(c) Many answers. Example: "Think of a number. Multiply by 3. Add 15. Divide by 3. Subtract the number you started with." Proof: $\tfrac{3x + 15}{3} - x = x + 5 - x = 5$. $\square$ (The trick: arrange the steps so the $x$ is cancelled at the end.)

### 23.

(a) Split a square of side $a + b$ by cutting each side into pieces $a$ and $b$. You get a square $a$ by $a$, a square $b$ by $b$, and **two** rectangles $a$ by $b$. Total area: $a^2 + 2ab + b^2$.

(b) $103^2 = (100 + 3)^2 = 10\,000 + 600 + 9 = 10\,609$. $98^2 = (100 - 2)^2 = 10\,000 - 400 + 4 = 9604$.

(c) $97\cdot 103 = (100 - 3)(100 + 3) = 10\,000 - 9 = 9991$.

(d) $a = b = 1$: $(1 + 1)^2 = 4$ but $1^2 + 1^2 = 2$. In the picture, he has forgotten the two rectangles $ab$.

### 24.

We need whole numbers $x, y \ge 0$ with $3x + 5y = 41$. Try each $y$ from 0 to 8 (since $5\cdot 9 > 41$) and see whether $41 - 5y$ is divisible by 3:

| $y$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $41 - 5y$ | 41 | 36 | 31 | 26 | 21 | 16 | 11 | 6 | 1 |
| divisible by 3? | no | **yes** | no | no | **yes** | no | no | **yes** | no |

So **(12 pens, 1 notebook), (7 pens, 4 notebooks), (2 pens, 7 notebooks)**.

Notice the pattern: the solutions differ by 5 pens and 3 notebooks, because swapping 5 pens (€15) for 3 notebooks (€15) keeps the total the same.

### 25.

(a) Write the sum $S$ forwards and backwards. The last term is $a + (n - 1)d$. Each column adds to $a + (a + (n-1)d) = 2a + (n - 1)d$, and there are $n$ columns:

$$
2S = n\,(2a + (n-1)d), \qquad S = \frac{n\,(2a + (n - 1)d)}{2}. \quad\square
$$

(b) $2027 = 1013 + 1014$. (Any odd number $2m + 1 \ge 3$ is $m + (m + 1)$.)

(c) Suppose $64 = m + (m + 1) + \dots + (m + k - 1)$ with $k \ge 2$ and $m \ge 1$. By (a) with $a = m$, $d = 1$: $64 = \dfrac{k(2m + k - 1)}{2}$, so $k(2m + k - 1) = 128$.

The two factors $k$ and $2m + k - 1$ differ by $2m - 1$, which is odd, so one of them is odd and the other even. The odd one divides $128 = 2^7$, so it must be 1. But $k \ge 2$ and $2m + k - 1 \ge 2 + 2 - 1 = 3$, so neither factor is 1. Contradiction. So 64 is not such a sum. $\square$

(The same argument shows that **no power of 2** is a sum of two or more consecutive positive whole numbers, and in fact every other positive number is. Try to prove that!)

### 26.

The number is $100a + 10b + c$ and its reverse is $100c + 10b + a$. The difference is

$$
(100a + 10b + c) - (100c + 10b + a) = 99a - 99c = 99(a - c),
$$

which is divisible by 99. $\square$

The difference is 0 exactly when $a = c$: the number is a palindrome, like 353 or 707.

### 27.

(a) There: $60 \div 30 = 2$ hours. Back: $60 \div 60 = 1$ hour. Total 120 km in 3 hours: **40 km/h**. The average is not 45 because you spend *more time* at the slow speed.

(b) Time there $\tfrac{d}{u}$, back $\tfrac{d}{w}$. Average speed:

$$
\frac{2d}{\frac{d}{u} + \frac{d}{w}} = \frac{2d}{\frac{dw + du}{uw}} = \frac{2d\cdot uw}{d(u + w)} = \frac{2uw}{u + w}. \quad\square
$$

(This is called the *harmonic mean* of $u$ and $w$.) Check with (a): $\tfrac{2\cdot 30\cdot 60}{90} = 40$ ✓.

(c) An average of 60 km/h over 120 km means 2 hours in total. But the way there already took 2 hours. So it's **impossible**, however fast you drive back. Algebraically: $\tfrac{2\cdot 30 w}{30 + w} = 60$ gives $60w = 1800 + 60w$, so $0 = 1800$: no solution.

### 28.

(a) Let the numbers be $x$ (larger) and $y$. Adding "$x + y = 20$" and "$x - y = 6$": $2x = 26$, $x = 13$, and then $y = 7$. Check: $13 + 7 = 20$, $13 - 7 = 6$ ✓.

(b) In the same way $2x = S + D$ and $2y = S - D$, so

$$
x = \frac{S + D}{2}, \qquad y = \frac{S - D}{2}.
$$

(The larger number is the average plus half the difference.)

### 29.

(a) $3x - 6 - 2x - 2 > x - 10$, so $x - 8 > x - 10$, so $-8 > -10$. That's always true, whatever $x$ is: **every number is a solution**.

(b) For example $x + 1 < x$. Subtracting $x$ gives $1 < 0$, which is false. No number is 1 more than itself and smaller than itself, so there are **no solutions**.
