# A1 Numbers and divisibility

[← Back to Stage A](../README.md)

**Stage:** A, groep 8 foundations
**School year:** groep 8 (2026–27); content from klas 1 (negative numbers) and beyond (number theory)
**Length:** 6 weeks at about 4 hours a week (about 24 hours)
**Prerequisites:** groep 7–8 arithmetic: the four operations with whole numbers, long division, times tables. No earlier module.
**Next:** [A2 Fractions, ratios, percentages and powers](../A2-fractions-ratios-powers/)

## Objectives

By the end of this module he can:

1. Calculate with negative numbers (*negatieve getallen*) and integers (*gehele getallen*), and explain why "minus times minus is plus".
2. Apply the order of operations (*rekenvolgorde*, *volgorde van bewerkingen*) correctly, including powers and brackets.
3. Use the divisibility rules (*deelbaarheidsregels*) for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11, and **prove** why the rules for 3, 4, 9 and 11 work.
4. Recognise primes (*priemgetallen*), use the sieve of Eratosthenes, and write any number as a product of primes (*ontbinden in priemfactoren*).
5. Find the greatest common divisor (*grootste gemene deler*, ggd) and least common multiple (*kleinste gemene veelvoud*, kgv) by prime factorisation and by Euclid's algorithm (*algoritme van Euclides*), and use them in word problems.
6. Prove that there are infinitely many primes, and explain the proof to someone else.
7. Write a short, clear proof in full sentences.

## Curriculum mapping

- **Onderbouw kerndoelen:** 10 (*Getallen, grootheden en vergelijkingen*: integers, operations, order of operations) and 14 (*Denk- en werkwijzen*: reasoning and proof).
- **Upper-school exam programmes:** groundwork for Wiskunde B domain A (*vaardigheden*) and wiskunde techniek A2 (*getallen en variabelen*) and B3 (*redeneren en bewijzen*). Divisibility and primes are also the start of the [olympiad](../../olympiad/) number theory module O2.

## I can…

- [ ] add, subtract, multiply and divide negative numbers without mistakes, and explain why $(-a)\cdot(-b) = ab$
- [ ] evaluate expressions like $2 - 3\cdot(4 - 6)^2 \div 6$ correctly and explain the order I used
- [ ] test a number for divisibility by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 in my head
- [ ] prove the divisibility rule for 9 (and 3) using place value
- [ ] find the prime factorisation of numbers up to a few thousand
- [ ] count the divisors of a number from its prime factorisation
- [ ] find a gcd and an lcm in two ways, and know when a word problem needs which
- [ ] run Euclid's algorithm and explain why it works
- [ ] prove that there are infinitely many primes

## Exit criteria

Move on to A2 when he can:

- do practice problems 1–9 quickly and with no more than one slip;
- solve at least 6 of the 8 school-exam problems without hints;
- solve at least 4 of the challenge problems, including **one written proof** (problem 18, 19 or 20) that a parent can follow line by line;
- explain the proof that there are infinitely many primes aloud, without notes.
