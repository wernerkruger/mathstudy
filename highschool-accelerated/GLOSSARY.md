# Dutch–English glossary

[← Back to the track overview](README.md)

Dutch school and exam terms with their English equivalents, grouped by topic. Within each group, terms are in Dutch alphabetical order. Where Dutch and English usage differ in a way that matters, there is a note.

Notation differences to know from the start:

- Dutch uses a **decimal comma**: 3,14 means 3.14. In this track we write the English decimal point, but school books and exams use the comma.
- Dutch writes large numbers with a space or point between groups of three digits: 1 000 000 or 1.000.000.
- Dutch exams often write $\log$ with a base as ${}^{g}\log x$ (for example ${}^{2}\log 8 = 3$) where English writes $\log_2 8$.
- An interval such as $\langle 2, 5]$ in Dutch means $(2, 5]$ in English: Dutch uses angle brackets for open ends.

## Exam instruction words

| Dutch | English | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| algebraïsch | algebraically | Show working with algebra. A graphing calculator answer alone scores nothing. |
| bereken | calculate | A calculation must be shown; a calculator is allowed unless "exact" or "algebraïsch" is asked. |
| benader in twee decimalen | approximate to two decimal places | Give a decimal answer rounded to 2 decimals, e.g. 3.14. |
| beredeneer | reason / explain by reasoning | A logical argument, often without heavy calculation. |
| bewijs | prove | A complete, rigorous proof with reasons for each step. |
| bepaal | determine | Find the value; any valid method (including the calculator) is allowed. |
| exact | exact | No rounding: answers like $\sqrt{2}$, $\pi$, $\tfrac{1}{3}$, $\ln 5$. |

| los op | solve | Find all solutions. |
| onderzoek | investigate | Find out whether something holds, with justification. |
| rond af op gehelen | round to whole numbers | Round to the nearest integer. |
| schets | sketch | A sketch with the important features (intercepts, extremes, asymptotes). |
| teken | draw | An accurate drawing. |
