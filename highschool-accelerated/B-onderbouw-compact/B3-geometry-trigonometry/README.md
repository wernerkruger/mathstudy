- **Length:** 9 weeks at about 4 hours per week

## Prerequisites

- [A4 Geometry and measurement](../../A-groep8-foundations/A4-geometry-measurement/): angles, area and volume of basic shapes, measuring and constructing with ruler, protractor and compass.
- [B2 Algebra and quadratics](../B2-algebra-quadratics/): square roots (*wortels*), simplifying $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$, solving linear and simple quadratic equations. This module can run in parallel with the last weeks of B2; sections 1–6 need almost no algebra.

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

1. Reason with angles: vertically opposite angles (*overstaande hoeken*), corresponding angles (*F-hoeken*) and alternate angles (*Z-hoeken*) at parallel lines, the angle sum of a triangle and of a polygon, and the exterior angle (*buitenhoek*) theorem.
2. Explain what a proof (*bewijs*) is, and write one with a clear given (*gegeven*), to prove (*te bewijzen*) and a chain of justified steps.
3. Use the congruence conditions (*congruentiegevallen*) SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and RHS (Dutch: ZZZ, ZHZ, HZH, ZHH, ZZR) and explain why SSA is not one.
4. Prove the isosceles triangle (*gelijkbenige driehoek*) theorem and its converse, the perpendicular bisector (*middelloodlijn*) property, and Thales' theorem (*stelling van Thales*) and its converse.
5. Prove Pythagoras' theorem (*stelling van Pythagoras*) in at least three ways, prove its converse, and work with Pythagorean triples.
6. Use similarity (*gelijkvormigheid*): the conditions hh, zhz and zzz, the scale factor (*vergrotingsfactor*) $k$, area factor $k^2$ and volume factor $k^3$, and the X-figure (*zandloperfiguur*) and A-figure (*snavelfiguur*).
7. Use sine, cosine and tangent (*sinus, cosinus, tangens*; *SOS-CAS-TOA*) in right triangles to find sides and angles, explain why these ratios depend only on the angle, know the exact values for 30°, 45° and 60°, and use $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1$ and $\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$.
8. Solve 3D problems: the space diagonal (*lichaamsdiagonaal*) of a cuboid, distances and angles in pyramids and cuboids, sight lines (*kijklijnen*) and blind spots (*dode hoek*), and simple views (*aanzichten*).

## Mapping to Dutch curriculum and exams

- **Onderbouw kerndoel 13** (geometry, *meetkunde*): 2D and 3D shapes, views and sight lines; the havo-vwo addition on reasoning with angles and the goniometric ratios in right triangles.
- **Onderbouw kerndoel 14** (ways of thinking and working, *denk- en werkwijzen*): reasoning and proof.
- **Wiskunde B (VWO exam programme):** domain E (*Meetkunde met coördinaten*), especially E1 (*meetkundige vaardigheden*); the skills A6 (*meetkunde*) and B3 (*redeneren en bewijzen*, plane-geometry proof). This module lays the proof foundations that domain E builds on.
- **Wiskunde D:** Thales' theorem and more circle geometry return later in module E3.

## I can…

- [ ] find unknown angles with vertically opposite, F- and Z-angles, and give the reason for each step;
- [ ] prove that the angles of a triangle add up to 180° and use $(n-2)\cdot 180°$ for polygons;
- [ ] state the five congruence conditions in English and Dutch, and show with an example why SSA fails;
- [ ] write a proof with *gegeven*, *te bewijzen* and *bewijs*, every step with a reason;
- [ ] prove the isosceles triangle theorem, its converse and Thales' theorem;
- [ ] prove Pythagoras' theorem in three ways and prove its converse;
- [ ] decide whether a triangle with given sides is right, acute or obtuse;
- [ ] recognise X- and A-figures and set up correct ratios;
- [ ] use area factor $k^2$ and volume factor $k^3$;
- [ ] explain why $\sin\alpha$, $\cos\alpha$ and $\tan\alpha$ depend only on $\alpha$;
- [ ] find sides and angles in right triangles with my calculator in degree mode;
- [ ] write down the exact values for 30°, 45° and 60° from two small triangles;
- [ ] compute the space diagonal of a cuboid and angles in pyramids;
- [ ] find a blind spot with sight lines and similar triangles.

## Exit criteria

You are ready to move on when you can:

1. Score at least 80% on a mixed timed test of school-exam-level problems (for example 15–23) without the notes, with correct rounding and units.
2. Write a clear two-column or prose proof (for example the isosceles triangle theorem, Thales' theorem or the converse of Pythagoras) where every step has a reason and the congruence condition is named.
3. Prove Pythagoras' theorem in two different ways from memory.
4. Derive the exact-value table for 30°, 45° and 60° from scratch in under five minutes.
5. Solve at least four of the seven challenge problems (24–30) with complete written arguments.
