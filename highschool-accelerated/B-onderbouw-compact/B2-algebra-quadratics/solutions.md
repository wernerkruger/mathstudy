## Practice

**1.**

(a) $3x(2x-5) = 6x^2-15x$

(b) $(x+4)(x-7) = x^2-7x+4x-28 = x^2-3x-28$

(c) $(2x-3)^2 = (2x)^2 - 2\cdot 2x\cdot 3 + 3^2 = 4x^2-12x+9$

(d) $(5a+2b)(5a-2b) = (5a)^2-(2b)^2 = 25a^2-4b^2$

(e) $(x+1)(x^2-x+1) = x^3-x^2+x+x^2-x+1 = x^3+1$. Almost everything cancels: this is the "sum of cubes" product.

**2.**

(a) $51^2 = (50+1)^2 = 2500+100+1 = 2601$

(b) $49^2 = (50-1)^2 = 2500-100+1 = 2401$

(c) $103\cdot 97 = (100+3)(100-3) = 10000-9 = 9991$

(d) $999^2 = (1000-1)^2 = 1000000-2000+1 = 998001$

**3.**

(a) $6x^2-9x = 3x(2x-3)$

(b) $4a^2b-10ab^2 = 2ab(2a-5b)$

(c) The common factor is $(x-3)$: $x(x-3)+5(x-3) = (x-3)(x+5)$

**4.**

(a) Product $12$, sum $7$: $3$ and $4$. So $(x+3)(x+4)$.

(b) Product $-15$, sum $-2$: $-5$ and $3$. So $(x-5)(x+3)$.

(c) Product $24$, sum $-11$: $-3$ and $-8$. So $(x-3)(x-8)$.

(d) Product $-56$, sum $1$: $8$ and $-7$. So $(x+8)(x-7)$.

**5.**

(a) $x^2-81 = (x+9)(x-9)$

(b) $4x^2-25y^2 = (2x)^2-(5y)^2 = (2x+5y)(2x-5y)$

(c) $3x^2-48 = 3(x^2-16) = 3(x+4)(x-4)$

(d) $x^4-16 = (x^2)^2-4^2 = (x^2+4)(x^2-4) = (x^2+4)(x+2)(x-2)$. The factor $x^2+4$ cannot be factorised further.

**6.**

(a) $ac = 6$, sum $7$: $6$ and $1$. $2x^2+6x+x+3 = 2x(x+3)+(x+3) = (2x+1)(x+3)$

(b) $ac = -12$, sum $-1$: $-4$ and $3$. $6x^2-4x+3x-2 = 2x(3x-2)+(3x-2) = (2x+1)(3x-2)$

(c) $ac = 24$, sum $-10$: $-6$ and $-4$. $3x^2-6x-4x+8 = 3x(x-2)-4(x-2) = (3x-4)(x-2)$

**7.**

(a) $x-4=0$ or $2x+3=0$, so $x=4$ or $x=-\frac32$.

(b) $x^2-7x=0 \Rightarrow x(x-7)=0 \Rightarrow x=0$ or $x=7$. (Do not divide by $x$: you would lose $x=0$.)

(c) $(x-8)(x+3)=0 \Rightarrow x=8$ or $x=-3$.

(d) $(2x-3)(2x+3)=0 \Rightarrow x=\frac32$ or $x=-\frac32$. (Or: $x^2=\frac94$.)

**8.**

(a) $x=7$ or $x=-7$.

(b) $x-3=\pm\sqrt{10}$, so $x=3+\sqrt{10}$ or $x=3-\sqrt{10}$.

(c) $2(x+1)^2=18 \Rightarrow (x+1)^2=9 \Rightarrow x+1=\pm3 \Rightarrow x=2$ or $x=-4$.

(d) $x^2=-4$. A square is never negative, so there are no solutions.

**9.**

(a) Half of $6$ is $3$: $x^2+6x-4 = (x+3)^2-9-4 = (x+3)^2-13$. So $p=3$, $q=-13$.

(b) $x^2-8x+5 = (x-4)^2-16+5 = (x-4)^2-11$. So $(x-4)^2=11$, $x = 4\pm\sqrt{11}$.

(c) $2x^2+12x+7 = 2(x^2+6x)+7 = 2\big((x+3)^2-9\big)+7 = 2(x+3)^2-11$.

**10.**

(a) $a=2$, $b=-3$, $c=-4$. $D = 9+32 = 41 > 0$: two solutions.

$$
x = \frac{3\pm\sqrt{41}}{4}
$$

(b) $D = 16-16 = 0$: one solution, $x = \frac{-4}{2} = -2$. (Indeed $x^2+4x+4 = (x+2)^2$.)

(c) $D = 1-24 = -23 < 0$: no solutions.

(d) $D = 4+4 = 8 > 0$: two solutions, $x = \frac{2\pm\sqrt8}{2} = \frac{2\pm2\sqrt2}{2} = 1\pm\sqrt2$.

**11.**

(a) $a=-2<0$, so it is a *bergparabool* (opens downwards).

(b) $x_{\text{top}} = -\frac{8}{2\cdot(-2)} = 2$, $y_{\text{top}} = -8+16+10 = 18$. Vertex $(2,18)$, axis of symmetry $x=2$. Check by completing the square: $-2(x^2-4x)+10 = -2\big((x-2)^2-4\big)+10 = -2(x-2)^2+18$.

(c) $y$-axis: $x=0$ gives $(0,10)$. $x$-axis: $-2x^2+8x+10=0 \Rightarrow x^2-4x-5=0 \Rightarrow (x-5)(x+1)=0$, so $(5,0)$ and $(-1,0)$. Note that $x=2$ lies exactly halfway between $-1$ and $5$.

(d) A downward parabola through $(-1,0)$, $(0,10)$, top $(2,18)$, $(4,10)$ (mirror image of $(0,10)$) and $(5,0)$.

(e) $-2x^2+8x+10 = 2x+10 \Rightarrow -2x^2+6x=0 \Rightarrow -2x(x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=0$ or $x=3$. Points $(0,10)$ and $(3,16)$.

**12.**

(a) $2x+1 = 3(x-3) = 3x-9 \Rightarrow x=10$. Check: $x-3=7\neq0$. Solution $x=10$.

(b) Multiply by $x$ (with $x\neq0$): $6 = x^2-x \Rightarrow x^2-x-6=0 \Rightarrow (x-3)(x+2)=0$. So $x=3$ or $x=-2$; neither is $0$, so both are solutions.

(c) Same denominators, so $x^2=9$ with $x\neq3$. Then $x=3$ or $x=-3$, but $x=3$ makes the denominator zero. Only solution: $x=-3$.

(d) Form $\frac AB=\frac AC$: $x^2-4=0$ or $x+1=2x-3$. So $x=2$, $x=-2$ or $x=4$. Check denominators: at $x=2$: $3$ and $1$; at $x=-2$: $-1$ and $-7$; at $x=4$: $5$ and $5$. None are zero, so all three are solutions.

**13.**

(a) $x^2-x-6=0 \Rightarrow (x-3)(x+2)=0 \Rightarrow x=3$ or $x=-2$. *Dalparabool*, below the axis between the roots: $-2<x<3$.

(b) $2x^2-5x-3\geq0$. Roots: $(2x+1)(x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=-\frac12$ or $x=3$. *Dalparabool*, on or above the axis outside the roots: $x\leq-\frac12$ or $x\geq3$.

(c) $D = 4-20 = -16<0$ and $a>0$, so the parabola lies entirely above the $x$-axis. The inequality holds for **all** $x$. (Or: $x^2+2x+5 = (x+1)^2+4 \geq 4 > 0$.)

## School-exam level

**14.**

(a) At $t=0$: $h=25$. The roof is $25$ m high.

(b) $t_{\text{top}} = -\frac{20}{2\cdot(-5)} = 2$, $h = -20+40+25 = 45$. Maximum height $45$ m after $2$ seconds.

(c) $-5t^2+20t+25=0 \Rightarrow t^2-4t-5=0 \Rightarrow (t-5)(t+1)=0$. Since $t\geq0$: after $5$ seconds.

(d) $-5t^2+20t+25>40 \Rightarrow -5t^2+20t-15>0 \Rightarrow t^2-4t+3<0 \Rightarrow (t-1)(t-3)<0$. So $1<t<3$: the ball is above $40$ m for $2$ seconds.

**15.**

(a) Two sides of $x$ m use $2x$ m, so the side parallel to the wall is $60-2x$ m. Area $A = x(60-2x)$.

(b) $A = -2x^2+60x$, a *bergparabool*. $x_{\text{top}} = -\frac{60}{2\cdot(-2)} = 15$, $A = 15\cdot30 = 450$. Maximum area $450\ \text{m}^2$, with sides $15$ m (perpendicular to the wall) and $30$ m (along the wall).

(c) $x(60-2x)=400 \Rightarrow -2x^2+60x-400=0 \Rightarrow x^2-30x+200=0 \Rightarrow (x-10)(x-20)=0$. So $x=10$ (enclosure $10$ by $40$) or $x=20$ (enclosure $20$ by $20$).

**16.** The outer rectangle measures $(12+2x)$ by $(8+2x)$.

$$
(12+2x)(8+2x)=140 \Rightarrow 96+40x+4x^2=140 \Rightarrow 4x^2+40x-44=0 \Rightarrow x^2+10x-11=0
$$

$(x+11)(x-1)=0$, and a width must be positive, so the path is $1$ m wide.

**17.** Let the numbers be $n$ and $n+2$ with $n$ odd. $n(n+2)=255 \Rightarrow n^2+2n-255=0 \Rightarrow (n-15)(n+17)=0$. So $n=15$ or $n=-17$. The pairs are $15, 17$ and $-17, -15$.

**18.**

(a) $D = p^2-36 = 0 \Rightarrow p=6$ or $p=-6$.

(b) $D<0 \Rightarrow p^2<36 \Rightarrow -6<p<6$.

(c) $x^2-3x+5 = x+p \Rightarrow x^2-4x+5-p=0$. Touching means exactly one intersection: $D = 16-4(5-p) = 4p-4 = 0$, so $p=1$. Then $x^2-4x+4=0$, $x=2$, $y=2+1=3$. Point of contact $(2,3)$.

**19.**

(a) After $n$ increases the price is $10+0.5n$ euros and $300-10n$ tickets are sold. Revenue = price times number: $R=(10+0.5n)(300-10n)$.

(b) $R = 3000-100n+150n-5n^2 = -5n^2+50n+3000$. $n_{\text{top}} = -\frac{50}{2\cdot(-5)} = 5$. Price $10+2.50 = $ €12.50, tickets $300-50 = 250$, revenue $12.50\cdot250 = $ €3125.

**20.**

(a) Time = distance / speed. At speed $v$ the trip takes $\frac{60}{v}$ hours; at $v+5$ it takes $\frac{60}{v+5}$ hours. The difference is $1$ hour.

(b) Multiply by $v(v+5)$ (both non-zero since $v>0$):

$$
60(v+5)-60v = v(v+5) \Rightarrow 300 = v^2+5v \Rightarrow v^2+5v-300=0 \Rightarrow (v-15)(v+20)=0
$$

Speed must be positive, so $v=15$ km/h. Check: $4$ hours versus $3$ hours.

**21.**

(a) The roots are $1$ and $5$, and the coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$, so $y=(x-1)(x-5)=x^2-6x+5$: $b=-6$, $c=5$. The vertex lies halfway between the roots at $x=3$: $y=(2)(-2)=-4$. Vertex $(3,-4)$.

(b) Vertex form: $y=a(x-2)^2-1$. Through $(0,3)$: $3=4a-1 \Rightarrow a=1$. So $y=(x-2)^2-1 = x^2-4x+3$.

**22.** Area $w(w+3)$ with $w>0$.

- At least $40$: $w^2+3w-40\geq0 \Rightarrow (w+8)(w-5)\geq0 \Rightarrow w\leq-8$ or $w\geq5$. With $w>0$: $w\geq5$.
- At most $70$: $w^2+3w-70\leq0 \Rightarrow (w+10)(w-7)\leq0 \Rightarrow -10\leq w\leq7$.

Both together: $5\leq w\leq7$ (width between $5$ and $7$ cm).

## Challenge

**23.**

(a) $(x^2+2)^2 = x^4+4x^2+4$, so $x^4+4 = (x^2+2)^2-4x^2 = (x^2+2)^2-(2x)^2$. Difference of squares:

$$
x^4+4 = (x^2+2+2x)(x^2+2-2x) = (x^2+2x+2)(x^2-2x+2)
$$

(b) By (a), $n^4+4 = (n^2+2n+2)(n^2-2n+2)$. The second factor is $(n-1)^2+1$.

- For $n=1$: $n^4+4=5$, which is prime.
- For $n\geq2$: $(n-1)^2+1\geq2$, and the first factor $n^2+2n+2$ is larger still. So $n^4+4$ is a product of two whole numbers that are both at least $2$, hence not prime.

So $n=1$ is the only one. (Check: $2^4+4=20$, $3^4+4=85=5\cdot17$, $4^4+4=260$.)

**24.** Suppose $n^2+60=m^2$ with $m$ a positive integer. Then $m^2-n^2=60$, so $(m-n)(m+n)=60$ with $0<m-n<m+n$.

The factors $m-n$ and $m+n$ differ by $2n$, an even number, so they have the same parity. Their product $60$ is even, so they cannot both be odd: both are even. The factor pairs of $60$ with both factors even and the first smaller are $2\cdot30$ and $6\cdot10$.

- $m-n=2$, $m+n=30$: $m=16$, $n=14$. Check: $196+60=256=16^2$.
- $m-n=6$, $m+n=10$: $m=8$, $n=2$. Check: $4+60=64=8^2$.

So $n=2$ or $n=14$.

**25.** Substitute $u=x^2-3x$: $u^2-2u-8=0 \Rightarrow (u-4)(u+2)=0$, so $u=4$ or $u=-2$.

- $x^2-3x=4 \Rightarrow x^2-3x-4=0 \Rightarrow (x-4)(x+1)=0 \Rightarrow x=4$ or $x=-1$.
- $x^2-3x=-2 \Rightarrow x^2-3x+2=0 \Rightarrow (x-1)(x-2)=0 \Rightarrow x=1$ or $x=2$.

Solutions: $x=-1$, $1$, $2$, $4$. Expanding first would give a degree-4 equation; the substitution keeps it quadratic.

**26.**

(a) Expand: $x^2-(a+b)x+ab-k^2=0$. The discriminant is

$$
D = (a+b)^2-4(ab-k^2) = a^2+2ab+b^2-4ab+4k^2 = (a-b)^2+4k^2
$$

A sum of two squares is never negative, so $D\geq0$ and there is at least one real solution. (There is exactly one only when $a=b$ and $k=0$.)

Alternative without the discriminant: the parabola $y=(x-a)(x-b)$ is a *dalparabool* through $(a,0)$, so its lowest value is at most $0\leq k^2$, and it grows without bound. So it must reach the height $k^2$ somewhere.

(b) Since $\sqrt p-\sqrt q$ is a real number, $(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\geq0$, so $p-2\sqrt{pq}+q\geq0$, i.e. $\frac{p+q}{2}\geq\sqrt{pq}$. Equality holds exactly when $\sqrt p-\sqrt q=0$, that is $p=q$.

For a rectangle with sides $p$ and $q$ and perimeter $P$: $p+q=\frac P2$. Then the area satisfies $pq\leq\left(\frac{p+q}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{P^2}{16}$, with equality only when $p=q$. So the square with side $\frac P4$ has the largest area.

**27.** By Vieta: $x_1+x_2=5$ and $x_1x_2=3$.

(a) $x_1^2+x_2^2 = (x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2 = 25-6 = 19$

(b) $\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2} = \frac{x_1+x_2}{x_1x_2} = \frac53$

(c) $(x_1-x_2)^2 = (x_1+x_2)^2-4x_1x_2 = 25-12 = 13$. (This is exactly the discriminant: $D=b^2-4ac$ with $a=1$.)

(d) $(x_1+x_2)^3 = x_1^3+3x_1^2x_2+3x_1x_2^2+x_2^3 = x_1^3+x_2^3+3x_1x_2(x_1+x_2)$, so $x_1^3+x_2^3 = 125-3\cdot3\cdot5 = 80$.

**28.** For the roots $x_1$, $x_2$ of $x^2-4x+1=0$: $x_1+x_2=4$, $x_1x_2=1$. The new roots $x_1^2$ and $x_2^2$ have

- sum $x_1^2+x_2^2 = 16-2 = 14$,
- product $x_1^2x_2^2 = (x_1x_2)^2 = 1$.

So the equation is $x^2-14x+1=0$. (Check: the roots of $x^2-4x+1$ are $2\pm\sqrt3$, and $(2\pm\sqrt3)^2 = 7\pm4\sqrt3$, which add up to $14$ and multiply to $49-48=1$.)

**29.**

(a) Square: $\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2 = x^2+2+\frac1{x^2} = 9$, so $x^2+\frac1{x^2}=7$.
Cube: $\left(x+\frac1x\right)^3 = x^3+3x+\frac3x+\frac1{x^3} = x^3+\frac1{x^3}+3\left(x+\frac1x\right)$, so $27 = x^3+\frac1{x^3}+9$ and $x^3+\frac1{x^3}=18$.

(b) Multiply by $x$ ($x\neq0$): $x^2+1=3x \Rightarrow x^2-3x+1=0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}$. Neither root is $0$.
Check with $x=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}$: then $\frac1x = \frac{2}{3+\sqrt5} = \frac{2(3-\sqrt5)}{9-5} = \frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}$ (the two roots multiply to $1$, by Vieta). Now $x^2 = \frac{14+6\sqrt5}{4}$ and $\frac1{x^2} = \frac{14-6\sqrt5}{4}$, which add up to $\frac{28}{4}=7$. Correct.

**30.**

(a) Two cases.

- $m=1$: the $x^2$-term disappears and the equation is linear: $-2x+3=0$, so $x=\frac32$. Exactly one solution.
- $m\neq1$: a quadratic equation with $D = 4m^2-4(m-1)(m+2) = 4m^2-4(m^2+m-2) = -4m+8$. Exactly one solution when $D=0$, so $m=2$. Then $x^2-4x+4=0$, $x=2$.

Answer: $m=1$ or $m=2$. The trap is forgetting that the abc-formula only applies when the coefficient of $x^2$ is not zero.

(b) $x^2+1=mx \Rightarrow x^2-mx+1=0$. Touching: $D=m^2-4=0$, so $m=2$ or $m=-2$.

- $m=2$: $x^2-2x+1=0$, $x=1$, point $(1,2)$.
- $m=-2$: $x^2+2x+1=0$, $x=-1$, point $(-1,2)$.

The two tangent lines are mirror images in the $y$-axis, as you would expect from the symmetry of $y=x^2+1$.
