## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

1. Use the rules for powers (*rekenregels voor machten*) $a^p \cdot a^q = a^{p+q}$, $a^p / a^q = a^{p-q}$, $(a^p)^q = a^{pq}$ and $(ab)^p = a^p b^p$, and explain each rule by counting factors.
2. Explain why $a^0 = 1$ and $a^{-n} = 1/a^n$ (*negatieve exponenten*) are forced if the rules are to keep working.
3. Work with square roots (*wortels*): simplify them (*wortels vereenvoudigen*, $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$), multiply and divide them, and rationalise the denominator (*wortel uit de noemer halen*).
4. Use $n$-th roots and fractional exponents $a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$ and $a^{p/q}$.
5. Prove that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
6. Write numbers in scientific notation (*wetenschappelijke notatie*) and calculate with them.
7. Model exponential growth and decay (*exponentiële groei / afname*) with $N = b \cdot g^t$, using the start value (*beginwaarde*) $b$ and the growth factor (*groeifactor*) $g$, and switch between $g$ and the percentage change per period (*procentuele toename/afname per tijdseenheid*).
8. Convert growth factors between time units, for example $g_{\text{week}} = g_{\text{day}}^7$ and $g_{\text{hour}} = g_{\text{day}}^{1/24}$.
9. Find doubling time and half-life (*verdubbelingstijd, halveringstijd*) by trial, table or graph.
10. Decide from a table whether a relation is linear (constant difference) or exponential (constant ratio).
11. Sketch and describe power functions (*machtsfuncties*) $y = a x^n$ for $n = 2, 3, 4, -1, -2, \tfrac12$: shape, symmetry, the hyperbola (*hyperbool*), asymptotes (*asymptoot*) and inverse proportionality (*omgekeerd evenredig*).
12. Read periodic graphs (*periodieke verbanden*): period (*periode*), amplitude (*amplitude*) and equilibrium line (*evenwichtsstand*).

## I can…

- [ ] explain each rule for powers by writing out the factors, and use the rules in both directions;
- [ ] explain why $2^0 = 1$ and $2^{-3} = \tfrac18$, without saying "because that is the rule";
- [ ] tell $(-2)^4$ from $-2^4$, and know why $2^3 \cdot 3^2 \neq 6^5$;
- [ ] simplify $\sqrt{72}$, rationalise $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}}$ and explain why $\sqrt{9+16} \neq \sqrt9 + \sqrt{16}$;
- [ ] calculate $27^{2/3}$ and $32^{-2/5}$ in my head;
- [ ] write the proof that $\sqrt2$ is irrational from memory;
- [ ] write numbers and products in scientific notation;
- [ ] set up $N = b \cdot g^t$ from a story and turn "decreases by 15% per year" into $g = 0.85$;
- [ ] convert a growth factor per day into one per week or per hour;
- [ ] find a doubling time or half-life to one decimal with a table;
- [ ] decide whether a table is linear, exponential or neither;
- [ ] sketch $y = x^2, x^3, x^4, x^{-1}, x^{-2}, \sqrt{x}$ and name their symmetry and asymptotes;
- [ ] read the period, amplitude and equilibrium line from a periodic graph or table.

## Exit criteria

Move on to the next module when:

1. you can do Practice problems 1–12 without the notes, with at most a few slips;
2. you score at least 80% on the School-exam level problems 13–21 under test conditions (calculator allowed, 90 minutes, no notes);
3. you have made a serious attempt at, and written up, at least four Challenge problems, including problem 24 (proving $\sqrt3$ irrational);
4. you can explain to someone else, in your own words, why $a^{-n} = 1/a^n$ and why a constant ratio in a table means exponential growth.
