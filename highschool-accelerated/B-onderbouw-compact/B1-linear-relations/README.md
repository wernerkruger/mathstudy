## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:

1. Recognise a linear formula (*lineaire formule*) $y = ax + b$ in a table, a graph, a formula and a story, and move between these four views.
2. Read and explain the slope (*richtingscoëfficiënt*, often written *rc*) and the start value (*startgetal*), and prove that the graph of $y = ax + b$ is a straight line.
3. Find the formula of the line through two points, and of lines that are parallel (*evenwijdig*) or perpendicular (*loodrecht*) to a given line, including a proof of the rule $a_1 \cdot a_2 = -1$.
4. Solve linear equations (*lineaire vergelijkingen*) with the balance method (*balansmethode*), including equations with brackets and fractions, and recognise equations with no solution or with every number as a solution.
5. Solve linear inequalities (*ongelijkheden*) and explain why the inequality sign flips when you multiply or divide by a negative number.
6. Solve systems of two linear equations (*stelsel van vergelijkingen*) by substitution (*substitueren*), by elimination (*combineren*, also called *optellen en aftrekken*) and graphically (the intersection point, *snijpunt*), and explain why a system has 0, 1 or infinitely many solutions.
7. Model (*modelleren*) real situations, such as phone plans, taxi fares, temperature conversion and break-even points, with linear formulas, equations and inequalities.

## I can…

- [ ] make a table and draw the graph for a formula such as $y = -2x + 3$
- [ ] read the slope and the start value from a formula, a table and a graph
- [ ] explain with similar triangles why the slope is the same between any two points of a line
- [ ] explain why the graph of $y = ax + b$ is a straight line
- [ ] find the formula of the line through two given points
- [ ] find the formula of a line parallel or perpendicular to a given line through a given point
- [ ] prove that perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is $-1$
- [ ] solve linear equations with brackets and fractions using the balance method
- [ ] recognise an equation with no solution and one that every number solves
- [ ] solve linear inequalities and explain why the sign flips when multiplying by a negative number
- [ ] solve a system of two equations by substitution, by elimination and graphically
- [ ] explain why a system has 0, 1 or infinitely many solutions, and tell which case applies before solving
- [ ] explain why elimination never loses or gains solutions
- [ ] set up and solve a linear model for a context and answer the question in words, with units and sensible rounding

## Exit criteria

Move on to the next module when all of the following are true:

1. You solve the school-exam-level problems (14–22) with at least 80% accuracy, unaided and without a calculator except where a context needs one.
2. You can explain out loud, without notes, why elimination works (the new system has exactly the same solutions as the old one) and why the sign flips in an inequality.
3. You can reproduce one proof of the perpendicular rule $a_1 \cdot a_2 = -1$.
4. You have made a serious attempt at every challenge problem and fully solved at least four of them.
5. Every box in the "I can…" list is ticked.
